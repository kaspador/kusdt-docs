KasPool

KasPool (bridge/kaspa/contracts/pool.ag) is a constant-product pool for KAS and kUSDT that runs as covenants on Kaspa L1. It is its own app, in its own covenant family, and it observes the kUSDT family. Its covenant ID is the deployment’s poolId; wallets pin it like kUSDT’s. Specification: SPEC §15.

Actor What it is
Pool A singleton. Its UTXO holds the KAS reserve, a fixed base deposit and the protocol’s accrued KAS. Its state also holds the circuit breaker and the pause
Position One per liquidity deposit: a number of shares and an owner key. Each carries 2 KAS

The pool’s kUSDT reserve is one KCC20 state in the kUSDT family, owned by the pool’s covenant ID (owner scheme 0x04), with borrowing disabled, holding Y + protocol_tokens.

Entries at a glance

Section titled “Entries at a glance”
Entry Does Checks (exact, with 128-bit products)
init First deposit X = Y = S = 0 before; S = kas_in; the Position gets kas_in − 1000 shares, and the 1000 unowned shares stay forever
buy KAS in, kUSDT out Not paused; fee 0.30 %, of which 0.05 % of the input goes to protocol_kas; (X + 0.997·in) · Y′ ≥ X · Y
sell, sell_all kUSDT in, KAS out Not paused; the same, with the protocol cut in kUSDT; and the circuit breaker, with now_daa covered by the transaction’s lock time
add KAS and kUSDT in, a new Position Not paused; shares · X ≤ kas_in · S and shares · Y ≤ tokens_in · S
remove Burns a Position (its owner signs) Open while paused; kas_out · S ≤ shares · X, tokens_out · S ≤ shares · Y; S stays ≥ 1000
collect The admin takes protocol_kas and protocol_tokens Admin signature; X and Y unchanged
pause Stops swaps and new liquidity The guardian’s signature; no kUSDT input in the transaction
unpause Resumes them The admin’s signature; no kUSDT input
set_keys Hands the admin key, the guardian key or both over Admin signature; no kUSDT input
Position.transfer Gives a Position to a new owner Owner signature; no kUSDT input in the transaction

The details of each follow.

Field Meaning

MAX_OPERAND is about 23 billion KAS on the KAS side. MAX_SWAP_INPUT is about 2.3 million KAS for a buy, or about 230 million kUSDT for a sell.

The circuit breaker

Section titled “The circuit breaker”

kUSDT minted with stolen bridge keys (3 of 5) is real kUSDT to the pool: the same token, with the same covenant ID. A forged mint and a sell into the pool can land in the same block, before any monitor sees them. So nothing off-chain can stop such a dump. The pool limits it on-chain instead.

Rule Value
Window window_len DAA: 36 000, an hour at 10 BPS
Cap Within a window, sells take at most max_out_bps (1 000, 10 %) of the KAS reserve the window opened with, or of the reserve now if that is smaller. So withdrawals shrink the cap too
New window A sell after the window ran its course (or the first sell ever) opens a new one, with the reserve of that moment
Clock The sell names now_daa. Its lock time must cover it, and consensus holds the transaction back until the DAG reaches it, so the clock cannot run ahead. It may not go back before the open window’s start
Who can change it Nobody: both values are fixed at genesis

Buys are not capped: they bring KAS in. Then the monitor pauses the pool with everything else. A dump costs the liquidity providers at most about 10 % of the KAS side, and they can still withdraw.

The local end-to-end run (npm run local:e2e) shows it: three stolen signer keys mint 400 000 kUSDT and sell them into the pool at once. The breaker refuses the whole sell, and the most it lets out is under 10 % of the pool’s KAS. Within seconds the monitor pauses the pool.

Pause

Section titled “Pause”
Who Entry Effect
The guardian (the bridge’s, held by the monitor) pause Swaps (buy, sell, sell_all) and new liquidity (init, add) stop
The admin unpause They resume

remove stays open while paused, so liquidity providers can always withdraw. The monitor pauses the pool on any alarm, with the vaults and the minter.

Liquidity shares

Section titled “Liquidity shares”
Entry Rule
init (first deposit) Requires an empty pool. Sets the price as kas_in : tokens_in. total_shares = kas_in, of which the depositor’s Position gets kas_in − 1 000 and 1 000 belong to nobody. Requires kas_in > 1 000
add Adds kas_in and tokens_in for shares new shares, with shares × X ≤ kas_in × S and shares × Y ≤ tokens_in × S. The depositor gets a new Position
remove Burns a Position (its owner signs) for at most its share: kas_out × S ≤ shares × X and tokens_out × S ≤ shares × Y. At least 1 000 shares must remain, and both reserves stay above zero

init checks no key: whoever makes the first deposit into a new pool sets its starting price. Adding liquidity pays no fee. A Position can change hands with its owner’s signature (Position.transfer).

Why MIN_LIQUIDITY

Section titled “Why MIN_LIQUIDITY”

The first deposit mints 1 000 shares that nobody owns and nobody can withdraw, because remove always leaves at least 1 000 shares. In the contract’s words, they keep the share price from being manipulated while the pool is tiny. Separately, remove requires both reserves to stay above zero.

Exact 128-bit products

Section titled “Exact 128-bit products”

Kaspa script numbers are 64-bit, but reserve products are not: 1 billion KAS against 100 million kUSDT is 10¹⁷ × 10¹⁴ = 10³¹. KasPool therefore compares a × b ≥ c × d exactly, without overflow:

  1. Each operand must be between 0 and 2⁶¹ − 1.
  2. Each is split into 31-bit limbs: a = a1 × 2³¹ + a0.
  3. The partial products (a0 × b0, a1 × b0 + a0 × b1, a1 × b1) each fit in 64 bits.
  4. Carries are normalised so both sides have limbs below 2³¹, and the limbs are compared from the top.

A test runs a buy against 1 billion KAS and 100 million kUSDT and confirms that the quote passes and one unit more fails.

Why it cannot be sandwiched

Section titled “Why it cannot be sandwiched”

A swap transaction spends the exact pool UTXO it was built on, and it names the exact amount the user receives. A competing swap that lands first spends that UTXO, so the user’s transaction becomes invalid instead of executing at a worse price. Nobody can insert a trade before and after a signed swap.

The cost is contention: only one swap can spend a given pool state. When several users swap at once, all but one must re-quote and re-sign. Wallets build on the newest pool state, mempool included (the coordinator API serves it), and rebuild when it moves.

A front-runner can still make a wallet rebuild on a worse price. So wallets never take a worse price without asking: they go ahead on their own only if the new quote is at least the one you confirmed. A worse one needs your approval again. Your slippage minimum stays the hard floor. (The wallet used to re-quote by itself within your slippage; the fix is being built.)

Why every entry guards the reserve

Section titled “Why every entry guards the reserve”

The reserve is owned by the pool’s covenant ID. Under KCC-2’s covenant-id/v1 scheme, any input of the pool’s family in a transaction counts as the pool approving, and that includes a Position. So two rules close every path:

Entry Guard
Every Pool entry that moves kUSDT (init, buy, sell, sell_all, add, remove, collect) Observes the kUSDT family exhaustively: only the reserve and the one user state the entry’s shape allows may move, and the reserve’s successor is forced
Pool.pause, Pool.unpause, Pool.set_keys Require OpCovInputCount(token_covid) == 0: no kUSDT may move in the transaction
Position.transfer Requires OpCovInputCount(token_covid) == 0

Tests prove that a thief who owns a Position, or who holds the admin key, cannot move the reserve: the guard itself rejects the transaction.

Protocol fees and the admin

Section titled “Protocol fees and the admin”
Entry Who Effect
collect The admin key Takes all accrued protocol fees: the KAS leaves the pool UTXO to outputs the admin signs for, and the kUSDT goes to a fees state. Reserves are unchanged
unpause The admin key Resumes a pool the guardian paused
set_keys The admin key Hands the admin key, the guardian key or both to other keys (it replaced set_admin)

The admin can take only the accrued protocol fees, resume a paused pool and hand the keys over; it cannot touch X, Y, any position or the circuit breaker. Before mainnet, the operator holds the admin key offline (mainnet checklist, item 7).

Tests

Section titled “Tests”

20 tests run the pool in Kaspa’s script engine (bridge/kaspa/tests/pool.rs). Each transaction spends the pool and its kUSDT reserve together, two covenant families, and runs input by input:

Area What is shown
First deposit Sets the price and locks the minimum liquidity; only once
Swaps Buys and sells pay at most the quote; one unit more is rejected; a round trip loses only the fees
Forced successors A pool claiming more tokens than the reserve keeps, a reserve keeping fewer than the pool records, or a pool forgetting the protocol’s cut is rejected
Fees Only the admin collects protocol fees
Reserve guards The reserve cannot ride along a Position transfer or an admin key change
Large numbers Exact 128-bit products with reserves whose product is far above 2⁶³
Liquidity Liquidity comes in proportion and leaves with its share; only the Position’s owner can burn it; Positions change hands with the owner’s signature
Circuit breaker Sells are capped within a window; a window reopens with the reserve of that moment; the clock cannot run ahead of the lock time or back; withdrawals shrink the cap
Pause and keys Only the guardian pauses and only the admin resumes; withdrawals stay open while paused; the reserve cannot ride along a pause or a key change; the admin rotates the guardian

tests/standard.rs builds the pool’s transactions with the real builders: a sell is about 108 000 transient mass, an add about 114 000. See Storage mass.

The full pool flow (genesis, first deposit, buy, sell, add, remove, collect) also ran on a kaspad v2.1.0 simnet.
