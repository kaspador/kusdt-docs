Status

As of 7 November 2026. The specification is v1, for testnet. kUSDT is deployed on testnet (TN10, Sepolia and BSC testnet), and the public testnet is open at testnet.trestly.io, with test coins only. The latest testnet release is v0.9.12, with wallet 0.9.8. The mainnet candidate is mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.11, a pre-release and not audited. The public testnet’s services run commit 503a292, with the third and fourth reviews’ fixes, and its coordinator also has the mint retry fix (c7ac3d1). Its vaults predate the payout wait, which needs a new testnet deployment, so payouts there don’t wait yet. Nothing is on mainnet. To try it, see Test on the public testnet.

Not audited

No external audit has taken place. Mainnet requires audits and the other steps in the mainnet checklist.

What the simnet run shows

Section titled “What the simnet run shows”

A local Kaspa node (kaspad v2.1.0, simnet) accepted every step of the Kaspa-side flow, bridge and pool:

  1. a KCC-23 preparation transaction carrying the token metadata;
  2. the genesis transaction that creates BridgeMint and a TokenSeed;
  3. a mint of two Ethereum deposits (nonces 0 and 1), signed by signers 0, 2 and 4;
  4. a KCC-20 transfer between two users;
  5. a redemption burn to a burn owner naming an Ethereum address, with the redemption payload;
  6. a mint of two BNB Chain deposits (nonces 0 and 1), signed by signers 1, 3 and 4;
  7. the pool’s genesis, then its first deposit (200 KAS and 20 kUSDT);
  8. a buy, a sell, a liquidity deposit, a liquidity withdrawal, and the admin collecting the protocol fees.

The whole bridge on one machine

Section titled “The whole bridge on one machine”

The local end-to-end run first passed on 3 November 2026. It has passed with the pool attack below since 4 November 2026, most recently on 7 November 2026. It runs two Hardhat chains (chain IDs 31337 and 31338, standing in for Ethereum and BNB Chain, with MockUSDT at 6 and 18 decimals and a vault each), the kaspad simnet, the 5 signers, the coordinator and the monitor, all on one machine. It showed:

  1. a deposit on each chain, minted on Kaspa with 3 of 5 signatures;
  2. a burn naming chain 2, released by its vault: 49.5 USDT paid for 50 kUSDT burned;
  3. healthy reserves (INV-1) afterwards;
  4. the coordinator’s pool and position APIs following a buy and a liquidity add and remove;
  5. the monitor agreeing with the coordinator before the attack: minted 299, released 50, owed 249 ≤ backing 249 (USDT), no alarms.

Then the attack. Three stolen signer keys try to mint 1 000 000 kUSDT; the mint allowance refuses it. They mint 400 000 kUSDT with no deposit; the covenant accepts it, because the signatures are valid and it is within the allowance. Selling all of it into the pool at once is refused by the pool’s circuit breaker, which lets out under 10 % of the pool’s KAS. Within seconds the monitor raises inv1 and pauses both vaults, the minter and the pool. The coordinator follows the forged mint and the pause, and a paused vault refuses a new deposit. This is the strongest evidence so far that the defences work together. See The monitor.

The wallet extension was also tested live against the same stack (npm run local:stack): deposits on both chains, a send, a redemption through to its release, swaps, and adding and removing liquidity.
