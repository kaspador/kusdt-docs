Coordinator API

Where it runs

The coordinator’s API process (kusdt api, bridge/services/src/api/) serves this API for mainnet at https://<the API address on the Status page>/api/v1, and in the local stack at http://127.0.0.1:9100/api/v1 (see Run the simnet flow). The mainnet address is published on Status: Addresses once the launch is verified. The public testnet serves it at https://testnet.trestly.io/api/v1, for testing integrations (Test on the public testnet).

The API is public and read-mostly: JSON over HTTPS under /api/v1. Nothing in it moves funds. Every answer that matters can be checked against a node: a token state against its UTXO, a pool state against its script.

Amounts are JSON integers, and some exceed 2⁵³, so clients must parse them exactly (not as JavaScript numbers). A value of 16 or more digits can also arrive as a decimal string, because the coordinator reads its saved state that way: the minter’s remaining is one. genesisRemaining, every breaker and exact value, and each tier’s above are always strings. Accept both forms. In a request body, the coordinator reads an integer exactly in any JSON notation: 12345678901234567, 12345678901234567.0 and 1.2345678901234567e16 are the same integer. A deployment’s services may lag this page (see Status for the release each one runs).

Where the answers come from

Section titled “Where the answers come from”

Since the work after round 12 the API is answered by a process of its own beside the coordinator, never by the coordinator itself: a flood of requests can at worst slow or stop these answers, and costs the process that mints and pays nothing. That process reads what the coordinator writes: its state, its token index and positions, its archive of closed records, and a public copy written once a second (the pool and the minter as followed now, how far each chain was read, the coordinator’s last health check). It asks the node and the EVM RPCs over connections of its own. The paths, the fields and the status codes are the ones they always were. How old an answer can be:

  • GET /health is the coordinator’s last health check, with its own at. It answers 503 when the public copy is more than 15 seconds old: the coordinator is stopped, restarting or cannot write, and every answer here is that old. It also answers 503 when the coordinator’s Kaspa follower has not seen its node move on for 60 seconds, when a chain has not been read in full for five minutes, and when the coordinator cannot write its state file: the copy says how long ago each follower last read its chain, and a quiet chain with nothing new still counts as read. A chain whose reads go through is still not followed when its blocks stand still across the coordinator’s RPCs: one RPC frozen or far behind holds the reads back to it, and /health answers 503 once the lowest head has not risen for five minutes (the lowest finalized block for 20 minutes on Ethereum, 10 on BNB Chain), or one RPC has trailed the best one by over two minutes for five, naming the chain and the RPC by its place in the coordinator’s list. The Kaspa follower counts only while its node calls itself synced and its virtual DAA keeps a tenth of the network’s pace (since round 16). When the node answers and the follower’s reads of the chain do not come back, the 503 says so, where it used to say the node was down (since run 20). Since rc.22 it also answers 503 when no payout has had three signatures for five minutes because signers did not answer or answered that they cannot sign, naming those signers by their place in the coordinator’s list (signers that refuse a payout, a burn not deep enough for them yet, are judging it and do not count; since run 20 one that refuses because it holds itself, its daily limits counted as spent for up to a day after a lost journal or a restore, does count). It says what each did in fixed words, “no answer”, the HTTP status it answered or “holds itself” with the time the hold ends, never the signer’s own error text, which can name the signer’s Kaspa node; the signer’s own /health and logs give the reason. Since round 18 a chain’s mints are counted the same way, per chain: for N min no mint on bsc has had three signatures: ..., once three signers have answered that chain’s mints with anything but a signature or a refusal for five minutes (an HTTP 500 when their RPCs disagree about a deposit, say). Until then only payouts were counted, and a chain’s mints could stop with nothing said for an hour. Since run 21 its warnings also name each payout or mint batch held for the signers’ daily limits: the payout or batch, its chain, the signers whose refusals started the hold and when it ends at the latest (R21-COORD2-1). Such a refusal starts a hold only with the signer’s signed proof that its limit is used up, checked against the signers’ agreed limit and what signers signed for the coordinator in the last 25 hours; any other is ordinary, and a warning names the signer and why its refusal was not believed. HEAD /health is answered as GET, without the body.
  • GET /pool answers 503 with retryAt once the copy is more than 15 seconds old, once the Kaspa follower has not seen its node move on for 60 seconds, and while the follower lags its node by more than 600 DAA (catching up after a start, or its reads of the chain failing), rather than hand a wallet a pool state the coordinator is not updating. Within those bounds it is the coordinator’s newest state, as before. Since the deep round of the second outside review of rc.22 (D4-3) it also answers 503 once the pool coin the coordinator follows has been missing from its node’s UTXO set for two minutes while its follower is caught up (a transaction it could not follow spent it, or a reorg undid a pool move it could not undo): every trade built on that coin would be refused, and /health is 503 naming the coin.
  • The records (deposits, redemptions, positions, the reserves’ figures, the minter in /status) are as of the coordinator’s last save, a few seconds behind what it knows. Every output /tokens and /positions list is still looked up on the node when it is answered. Since the deep round of the second outside review of rc.22 (D4-1) a reorg that drops a block undoes what that block changed in the token index: the outputs its transactions spent are listed again, and those they made are not. The journal behind it keeps the blocks of the last finalityDaa (1,000 DAA at least) and at most 2,000 spent outputs, the newest block always; until then only the minter’s own mints were undone, and a transfer a reorg dropped for good left its sender’s kUSDT out of /tokens.
  • Before the coordinator has written anything, the routes that need its files answer 503 with retryAt.

The coordinator learns the minter, the pool and the positions the way the monitor does: from accepted transactions, checking every successor against the outputs (see Following covenant states). It takes supply from the chain:

Calls

Section titled “Calls”
Call Answer
GET /status {network, kaspa: {covenantId, poolId, virtualDaa, minter: {utxo, state}, genesisRemaining, tokenValue, burnValue, finalityDaa, finalityTiers}, chains: [{chain, name, chainId, vault, token, decimals, scannedBlock, depositNonce, backing, unreservedBacking, reservedUnits, releaseAvailable, paused, fees: {feeBps, minDepositFee, minReleaseFee, minDeposit, maxDeposit, releaseCapacity, releaseRefillPerSecond}, blocklist}]}. Each vault’s parameters as it reports them. blocklist is the token view the bridge honours (isBlackListed), or null for a token that keeps none (BSC-USD). scannedBlock is the last final block the coordinator read (finalizedBlock is its old name). Since run 21 a vault’s figures (depositNonce, backing, unreservedBacking, reservedUnits, releaseAvailable, paused, fees, payoutDelay) are read at one block that every one of the API’s RPCs has (the lowest head, two blocks back) and shown only when all of them answer the same; until then all but fees were the first RPC’s word. While they disagree the entry holds the last state they agreed on, with staleSeconds (its age) and rpcsDisagree (the figures they answer differently); before they have agreed once, the call fails, as it does while the RPCs cannot be read. staleSeconds alone means the last read failed
GET /tokens?owner=<kaspa address or x-only hex> {states: [{utxo, state}], unread?}: the owner’s unspent kUSDT states. unread (since rc.19) lists apart the coordinator’s records of the owner’s states at an address that holds too many coins for the node’s answer to be read at all, with the KAS each coin carried as recorded: the node said neither that they are there nor that they are gone, so they are never in states (see Known limits). Answers 400 for an owner that is neither, and 503 with retryAt while the API process, just started, has not yet read the token index to its end (since round 14: it used to answer every owner states: []). A state whose owner is a script’s hash (P2SH: a multisig, a script of its own) is listed to that owner as a key’s is (since run 21; until then only one minted to it was, Daybreak [57]); a burn the transaction’s payload names is not, as it is a redemption. A burn with no payload is listed under its burn owner, which nobody holds a key for.
GET /pool {pool, reserve, pending: [txid], confirmed: {pool, reserve}, prices: {kasInKusdt, kusdtInKas}, exact: {unitsPerKas, sompiPerKusdt}, breaker, paused, tvlKas}. pool and reserve (each {utxo, state}) are the newest, mempool included, carried through the pool moves in pending (only moves a block can take: a pool transaction that no block will include is never listed, see Known limits); confirmed is the newest accepted. A reserve whose making move the coordinator followed also carries made, the id of that transaction, whose body GET /pool/made/:txid serves (not in confirmed; see Verifying answers). exact: {unitsPerKas, sompiPerKusdt} gives the spot prices as integer strings, rounded down (kUSDT units per KAS, sompi per kUSDT); prices are floats for display. breaker: {maxOutBps, windowLen, maxKasOutNow, windowEndsDaa, virtualDaa, poolDaa, minAgeDaa, readyAtDaa} is what sells may still take out of the KAS reserve now (see the circuit breaker): poolDaa is the DAA score the pool UTXO was accepted at (null while it is only in the mempool, and for a moment while the coordinator does not know it yet, see Request limits), minAgeDaa the least age a pool transaction must claim now, and readyAtDaa the DAA score before which none can be sent (null while the newest pool UTXO is only in the mempool, or while its age is not known and a claim is needed). paused says whether the guardian paused the pool. priceKas remains as an alias of prices.kasInKusdt. Answers 404 until the pool exists.
GET /pool/made/:txid {txid, made}: the body of transaction txid (64 lowercase hex digits) that made the pool reserve the coordinator follows now (the confirmed one or the newest), without its signature scripts and with its payload’s digest in place of the payload: {version, inputs: [{previousOutpoint, sequence}], outputs: [{value, scriptPublicKey, covenant}], lockTime, subnetworkId, gas, payloadDigest}. Any other id answers 404, from memory, with no read of the node. A body never changes, so the answer carries Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable: keep it by its id and fetch it once (kUSDT Wallet and the web app do). The per-client limit counts it like any other request. It is not in /pool itself because a trader chooses the making transaction’s outputs: about 1,200 fit a block, some 140 KB of body
GET /positions?owner=<kaspa address or x-only hex> {positions: [{utxo, state: {tokenCovid, shares, owner}}], unread?, truncated?, notListedAtOrBelowShares?}: the owner’s live liquidity positions, each found in the node’s UTXO set; unread as for /tokens. The coordinator lists 50,000 positions at most (since round 15): while that index is full, positions of notListedAtOrBelowShares shares or fewer may be missing, and the answer says truncated (see Known limits). Answers 400 for an owner that is neither, and 503 with retryAt while the API process, just started, has not yet read the positions to their end.
GET /deposits/:chain/:nonce {chain, nonce, txHash, blockNumber, logIndex, sender, ownerScheme, owner, units, fee, minted, status, kaspaTxid?, refund?}. A nonce the coordinator has not read yet answers only {chain, nonce, status: "pending"}. refund appears once Kaspa skipped the nonce: {chain, nonce, status, skipTxid, since, evmTxHash?, attempts?, nextTry?, lastError?}, with status skipped → refunded, or blocked while the token blocks the depositor (see Refunds). :chain is the code (1, 2), ethereum, bsc or bnb, or the deployment’s chain name; an unknown chain answers 404, and a nonce that is not a decimal integer from 0 to 2⁵³ - 1 written with no leading zero (5, not 005) 400
GET /recipients/:chain/:address {chain, address, blocklist, blocked, payable, reason}: whether the vault would pay address. payable is false for the zero address, the vault’s own address, and an address the token blocks; reason says which, or is null. Answers 503 when the token can’t be read, and 400 for an address that is not 0x and 40 hex digits. Wallets refuse a burn unless payable is true
GET /deposits?sender=0x… or ?owner=… {deposits: [{chain, ...}], total, limit, offset}: the matching deposits, newest first, at most 1 000 per answer. limit and offset page them; with no filter an answer holds 100 unless limit says more. sender is an EVM address (0x and 40 hex digits, in any case): anything else matches no deposit and gets an empty page
POST /redeems Body {transaction, transactionId} (a submitted burn in node JSON, with the ID the node answered) or {txid, outputIndex, chain, address, state} (the preimage of a burn without payload). state is {amount, owner, ownerScheme, borrowScheme, borrowGuard, extensionCommitment}. Answers {redeems: [{redeemId, status}]}. A malformed body answers 400, and one above 512 kB answers 413 (see Request limits), as does one whose text written out again by the API is over the 1.2 MB the coordinator reads of one exchange (since round 14). See Reporting a burn for what a report can and cannot do
GET /redeems/:txid/:outputIndex {redeemId, txid, outputIndex, chain, address, units, state, status, firstSeen, fee?, payout?, evmTxHash?, readyAt?, queuedTx?, attempts?, nextTry?, lastError?, note?, unverified?, checkedAt?, candidates?}, or 404 if no such redemption was seen. A txid that is not 64 hex digits, or an outputIndex that is not a decimal integer from 0 to 65535, answers 400 (an index that was not a number used to be read as 0, and answered output 0’s record). unverified marks a claim only a client has made so far, and candidates holds other claims waiting for the same outpoint. status queued means the payout is waiting in the vault: readyAt says until when (unix seconds) and queuedTx is the transaction that queued it. fee and payout come from the vault’s Released event; attempts, nextTry and lastError show the relayer’s retries, or why a payout is held
GET /reserves {supply: {minted, released, pendingBurns, circulating}, backing: {<chain name>: units, total}, at: {<chain name>: block}, healthy}, where healthy means total ≥ minted − released (INV-1). Each vault’s backing is read at the block its releases were counted to (at), so a release that just landed never looks like missing backing. circulating is minted − released − pendingBurns. pendingBurns counts burns not yet released, leaving out invalid records and claims neither the chain nor the node has shown, so a client’s word cannot move it. at is null for a vault whose releases have not been counted yet: its backing is then read at the newest block every one of the API’s RPCs has. Either way every RPC must answer the same (since run 21; the first RPC’s word was taken for such a vault before), or the call fails.

Status values

Section titled “Status values”
Object Statuses, in order
Deposit pending → final → minting → minted, or refunded
Refund (refund.status) skipped → refunded, or blocked (held while the token blocks the depositor; back to skipped once it doesn’t)
Redemption seen → final → signing → released, or invalid (not in the UTXO set), or unpayable (at or below the release fee, back to final if the fee falls below it; or a zero or vault recipient, for good), or blocked (held while the token blocks the recipient; back to final once it doesn’t)

A blocked record is checked again every hour (blockedRecheckMs). It never holds up other records. See Blocked recipients.

A payout the vault’s owner cancelled keeps its status, and its lastError says that it waits until the owner reinstates it. The coordinator asks no signer for it and sends nothing. It looks again every ten minutes and goes on once the owner has reinstated it. It holds up no other record.

Which burns are recorded. Only a plain burn is recorded as a redemption: a state with no borrowing and no extension, and an amount above zero. That is the signers’ own rule for a burn they would pay. A plain burn at or below the release fee is recorded and marked unpayable; the coordinator marks all of those at once, so they never take a real burn’s turn.

Request limits

Section titled “Request limits”

A request body is at most 512 kB (413 above it), and must arrive whole within 10 seconds or its connection is cut. The server reads at most 64 bodies at a time, 4 from one client (an IPv4 address, an IPv6 /64), 16 from one client network (an IPv4 /24, an IPv6 /48), and the last 16 only for a network that holds none: more answers 429. A request’s headers must arrive within 10 seconds and the whole request within 30. These limits are separate from the limit of requests per minute per client, which an operator turns on in the API process’s config (apiClientIp). A proxy in front can add its own: the mainnet package’s Caddy allows bodies up to 1 MB and limits how long a request may take to arrive.

The read routes have limits of their own (they kept the API process’s own answers going before the split, and do so still):

  • /tokens and /positions read the owner’s addresses on the node. One read per owner at a time, shared by everyone who asks; the answer is kept for a second, or for twenty times the time its read took, up to a minute. All these reads together have a budget of time. Past it an owner asked about lately gets the last answer with stale: true, and anyone else 503 or 429 with retryAt.
  • /pool reads no address. The pool output’s age (poolDaa) is the DAA score at which the coordinator’s own follower saw that output accepted, and it keeps it in its state (and its public copy). For an output it did not see accepted (the first start after an upgrade from before rc.18, a pool that has not moved since the launch, a reorg that undid a pool move) the pool’s address is read once, in the background, by the API process: until that read is back, /pool answers poolDaa: null, which claims no age, and it never waits for it. What it learns stays in the API process: the coordinator takes no word from the process that faces the public.
  • Everything the API asks the Kaspa node goes over connections of its own, in a process of its own. Its reads of addresses wait in a line of their own, at most eight of them (503 with retryAt past that). The loops’ reads never wait behind them.
  • The archive (closed deposits and redemptions, on disk) is read within a budget of time; 503 or 429 with retryAt past it. A list read from it is kept for 30 seconds at the most.
  • What is kept in memory is bounded by the records it holds and by its age: the owners’ answers (100,000 outputs in all), the lists read from the archive (20,000 records, an empty list counting as one), the pages of /deposits (20,000 deposits).

Verifying answers

Section titled “Verifying answers”
  • A token state from /tokens names its UTXO. Recompute its locking script with kusdt-tx script and compare it with the UTXO on your own node. The wallet does exactly this before it shows or spends a state.
  • A pool state from /pool, and a position from /positions, can be checked the same way.
  • The pool’s kUSDT reserve can be checked without its address, which anyone can fill and anyone can trade the reserve back to (run 21, R21-POOL-1). GET /pool/made/:txid, for the id reserve.made names, serves the body of the transaction that made it, as far as its id goes: {version, inputs: [{previousOutpoint, sequence}], outputs: [{value, scriptPublicKey, covenant}], lockTime, subnetworkId, gas, payloadDigest} (a version 1 id commits to the payload by its digest alone, so the digest stands in for the payload). Check that its id (version 1: transactionIdOf in bridge/packages/kaspa/src/txid.ts, a port of rusty-kaspa’s hashing/tx.rs) is the reserve’s transactionId, and that the reserve is its first output bound to kUSDT’s covenant ID, at the script of the reserve’s state, in a transaction that also made a coin of the pool’s (reserveMadeBy in bridge/packages/protocol/src/pool-follow.ts). When that transaction also made the pool coin you find on your node, that is the reserve, with the KAS its output holds, unspent while the pool coin is: a pool entry either refuses kUSDT beside it or makes the reserve’s successor as its first kUSDT output, so the reserve is spent only with the pool coin of its time. A reserve made before the pool coin (a tick or a pause since) the body shows as it was made, not as still there: list its address as well. kUSDT Wallet and the web app do so, take the body for such a reserve only when its address cannot be listed, and list the address when reserve.made is missing (a coordinator that has not followed the reserve’s last move). They keep each body by its id and fetch it once.
  • /reserves can be checked against the vaults’ backing(), read at the blocks in at, and the minter’s remaining. healthy compares the backing with everything minted and not yet released, so a burn still waiting for its release still counts as owed.

The monitor computes the same supply figures from its own nodes and does not use this API.

Reporting a burn

Section titled “Reporting a burn”

POST /redeems tells the coordinator about a burn it has not seen on its own, with its payload’s chain and address. The API process checks the body’s shape and holds the claim until the coordinator fetches it, which it does itself, at most five times a second and eight claims at a time, within the budget of time a claim always had. At most 32 claims (4 MB) wait at once, each for 15 seconds: past any of these, and while the coordinator is not asking (it is stopped or restarting), the answer is 503 with retryAt, and nothing is kept. A wallet asks again; a burn nobody reported is still found on the chain, unless the coordinator was rebuilt or re-anchored past it: it reads burns only from where it starts. So kUSDT Wallet keeps each burn’s claim and sends the same claim again while GET /redeems answers 404 for it, or while no claim of it was taken and the API cannot say: at once, then a minute later, each wait twice the one before and at most six hours, twenty times in all (since run 21 round 2, R21-COORD2-5). A burn its Kaspa node did not answer about (“May have been sent”) is claimed only once the node shows it accepted: a claim makes the burn show as pending, and one that never landed must not. Since rc.22, when the coordinator does ask and the API process refuses it for its token (the operators’ claims setting is wrong), the 503 says so instead, and that the burn is still found on the chain and paid without the claim. A report is a claim, not a fact:

  • A transaction a client posts can only add redemption claims. Nothing proves it is the transaction with that id, so it never changes the coordinator’s token index.
  • A deposit that names a burn owner needs a claim, unless it is the depositor’s own. Such a deposit is minted straight into a burn, and a mint carries no payload. Since run 21 the coordinator records the redemption itself when the owner is the burn owner of the depositor’s own address on chain 1 or 2. For any other burn owner it records none, and the burn is never paid, until someone posts {txid, outputIndex, chain, address, state} for the minted output (the mint’s transaction ID, the output’s index, the chain and address the burn owner names, and the minted state). The web app and the wallet never build such a deposit; whoever deposits that way sends the claim.
  • Nothing is read from the node on the request. A claim for an output the coordinator’s own follower has read from an accepted transaction (the exact state, with kUSDT’s covenant ID) is a burn at once and replaces whatever came first. Any other claim waits, and a loop of its own tries every waiting claim against the follower’s records and then against the node’s UTXO set; the one found wins. Before round 11 the claimed state’s address was listed on the request itself: an address of the caller’s choosing, holding any number of coins.
  • A burn the coordinator reads from the chain itself always replaces a different claim.
  • Unproved claims are capped, and fairly. The coordinator keeps at most 256 redemptions on a client’s word alone (bulk claims read from a posted transaction counted apart). Since run 21, past that bound the client network that sent the most loses one first: what came to nothing, then what the node was asked about and did not show, then the newest of its others. Until then the oldest went, whoever sent it, and 257 claims about made-up outputs from one address pushed out anyone’s (R21-IDENT-1). A claim the node was not asked about yet is never dropped to make room for a newer one from another network: when one claim from each of very many networks fills the bound, the next is refused with 503 and retryAt. A claim already found does not count, and an honest burn loses nothing, since the chain shows it anyway. Each pass looks up at most about 128 claims on the node, after everything the chain has shown. A claim that came to nothing stays visible for an hour.
  • Other claims about one output wait their turn. Each redemption keeps at most 16 other claims (candidates), one per client network, and the node is asked about each of them, whatever it answers about the others. Since run 21 a claim takes the place of one the node was already asked about (checkedAt), never of one it was not: until then false states sent after the real one pushed it out before the node was asked, and the burn was never paid (Daybreak [10]). While the places are full a network’s claim keeps its place for ten minutes after the node was asked about it: networks that sent again after every pass of the claims loop kept a newcomer out for good (run 21 review, RV-CK). A claim with no place for now (the places hold claims not yet asked about, or its network’s own claim is not) is refused with 503 and retryAt; send it again after that.
  • The coordinator’s record of a burn a claim names stays. Its book of burns read from the chain keeps the record of any output a waiting claim names, by its first state or any other, however many larger outputs anyone makes (Daybreak [40]).
  • Unproved claims are not supply. They are left out of pendingBurns in GET /reserves until the chain or the node shows them.

So reporting a wrong state first cannot strand someone else’s redemption (fixed 5 November 2026). Signers re-check every burn on their own nodes before they sign, whatever the coordinator says.
