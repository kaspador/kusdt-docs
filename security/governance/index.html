Governance

kUSDT has three governed parts. Each has its own rules, and none of them can move user funds outside the protocol’s paths.

BridgeMint (Kaspa): the signers

Section titled “BridgeMint (Kaspa): the signers”

BridgeMint is governed by the signers themselves, through one govern entry. Each action is signed over the govern digest, which includes the current admin_nonce, so every message is single-use.

Action Quorum Effect
set_value 4 of 5 Change the minter’s own KAS deposit
unpause 4 of 5 Resume minting, on a paused minter only. It takes 4 so that a bad minting quorum of 3 cannot undo the guardian’s pause
set_limits 4 of 5 New allowance ceiling and refill rate, each at most 10¹⁵ units (the lifetime cap). The allowance is settled at the old rate first, then lowered to the new ceiling if above it
set_signers 4 of 5 New signer set and guardian, effective immediately
propose_recovery 3 of 5 Propose a new signer set and guardian, for when two keys are lost for good. See Recovery
veto_recovery any 1 of 5 Cancel a proposed recovery
skip_nonce 4 of 5 Move one chain past its current nonce. The vault then refunds that deposit to its depositor, exactly what it minted, once 3 signers see the skip final on their own nodes (see Refunds)

Nothing can raise the hard cap. pause needs only the guardian.

A pause voids what was signed before it. A pause moves admin_nonce forward by one, so every 4-of-5 signature made before it stops counting. A governance signature has no expiry of its own: without this rule, an unpause that four signers signed once and never sent could be used after a later pause by anyone holding it and three keys. After a pause the signers sign again, with the new nonce, and the monitors are told the new line.

A governance action signs its age. Each one claims an age of the minter’s UTXO, and the mint allowance refills by that age. The signers sign the age (since rc.16), so a copy of the action sent with another age is not valid. The other side of that: the action can be sent only once the UTXO is that old, and a mint that lands first makes a new UTXO at age zero. So the signers agree the age before they sign, and sign an urgent action for age 0, which is always valid and credits no refill. What is then not credited is a delay only, and a known limit.

Recovery when two keys are lost

Section titled “Recovery when two keys are lost”

Every action above but the recovery needs 4 of 5. Two signers losing their keys for good would mean a minter pause could never be lifted and the lost signers never replaced, and the covenant can’t be upgraded. So three signers can propose a new signer set and guardian. Anyone can carry it out once the minter has stood untouched for 72 hours, which consensus enforces, and any single signer can veto it until then. A mint restarts the wait.

This is a trade-off: three stolen keys can attempt to replace the signers, and an honest signer has 72 hours to stop them. Every monitor raises an alarm on a proposal the operators didn’t announce.

A proposal and a veto spend the minter coin, and an unpause is signed for one coin (the paused one). So while a proposal stands, any one signer’s veto voids an unpause the four have signed, and they sign it again: a delay of one round of signing, nothing minted or lost. With a proposal standing, veto it or let it be carried out first, then sign the unpause.

KusdtVault (EVM): the owner

Section titled “KusdtVault (EVM): the owner”

Each vault has an owner. On mainnet the owner is KusdtAccessManager, OpenZeppelin’s AccessManager (5.1.0) with renounceRole closed, so nobody can give up a role at once, and revokeCanceller added (below). Its only admin is the project Safe; the vault itself is unchanged. Every owner call goes through the manager: the Safe schedules it, waits its delay, then executes it within a week. Calling the vault directly reverts.

Role Held by Delay What it may call
ADMIN The Safe only 48 hours The manager itself: roles, delays, guardians, which role may call what
GOVERNOR The Safe 48 hours setFees, setDepositSurge, setDepositLimits, setReleaseLimits, reinstateRelease, reinstateRefund, setPayoutDelay, setSigners, withdrawFees, transferOwnership
EMERGENCY The Safe 6 hours by default (1 to 48) setGuardian, unpause, cancelRelease, cancelRefund: a stolen guardian key costs hours, not days
PAUSER The Safe None pause. The guardian still pauses on its own
CANCELLER Independent cancellers Cancel any scheduled GOVERNOR or EMERGENCY call, and the Safe’s scheduled power-adding calls and revocations on the manager itself
GUARDED Nobody Nothing. The manager’s own power-adding functions and revokeRole are mapped to it, so that the cancellers can cancel them

One honest canceller stops a bad proposal. The cancellers are parties other than the Safe’s holders, and the monitor can hold a canceller key too, cancelling every queued call nobody announced. A canceller can only cancel: a rogue one can hold up vault calls, an emergency unpause included, until the Safe removes it, which takes 48 hours and which no canceller can cancel.

The cancellers can also cancel the Safe’s power-adding calls on the manager (since rc.16). A compromised Safe could schedule a change to the manager itself, for example a new GOVERNOR with no delay, and use it 48 hours later. Before, such a call could be seen but not cancelled. Now the manager’s functions that add power (grantRole, setRoleAdmin, setRoleGuardian, setGrantDelay, setTargetAdminDelay, setTargetFunctionRole, setTargetClosed, updateAuthority) are mapped to GUARDED, a role nobody holds, whose guardian is CANCELLER. That changes who may cancel them, not who may call them: only the Safe calls them, with its 48 hours, and any one canceller can cancel one while it waits.

Removing a canceller is deliberately not cancellable, and it is the only revocation that isn’t. If it were, one rogue canceller could cancel its own removal for ever. So a canceller is removed through a function of its own, revokeCanceller, which removes cancellers only and stays outside GUARDED: the Safe’s 48 hours, and only the Safe can cancel it. Every other revocation goes through revokeRole, which is mapped to GUARDED like the power-adding calls (since round 11). Before, revokeRole was left outside, and that covered the Safe’s own roles: stolen Safe keys could pause a vault and schedule the removal of every role the Safe holds, and 48 hours later nobody could ever unpause it, with no canceller able to stop that.

The result: whoever holds the Safe can add power or strip the Safe’s roles only once no canceller cancels it. It must remove every canceller, which takes 48 hours in plain sight and which no canceller can stop, or outlast them. Every monitor alarms the moment such a call is queued (owner-op-unannounced) and pauses everything. The removals of the cancellers are the signal for the people behind the Safe to act.

What that buys, in figures (corrected in round 12; bridge/evm/test/safe-compromise-times.test.ts):

  • A freeze takes one transaction, not 96 hours. The Safe holds PAUSER with no delay, and whoever scheduled an operation may cancel it, so a holder of the Safe’s keys pauses a vault at once and cancels every unpause the owners schedule through the same Safe. The vault stays paused for as long as they hold the keys. If they replace the Safe’s owners (one Safe transaction, with no delay, that the AccessManager never sees) the honest owners are out and it stays paused for good. No guardian and no canceller can unpause. What the cancellers stop is only the last step, taking the Safe’s own roles away so that the freeze outlasts the keys.
  • A theft takes 48 hours from the cancellers’ last cancel. The removals of the cancellers and the power-adding call can be scheduled in the same minute, and a cancelled call can be scheduled again in the next block. It is 96 hours only when a canceller cancels every re-schedule right up to its own removal at hour 48. With one cancel at hour 1 the signers are replaced at hour 49.
  • So the cancellers’ job does not end with one cancel. Each cancels every re-schedule, and cancels everything still pending just before its own removal can run. The Safe can queue many operations in one transaction and each cancel is a transaction of the canceller’s, so a canceller needs gas for many cancels, or a monitor holds a canceller key (cancellerEvmSecretPath) and cancels by itself.

It is a delay, not a stop. If nobody acts in that time, stolen Safe keys still win, and the pause they can set at any moment gives users no way out in the meantime. No service watches the Safe’s own owner list yet (the Safe’s AddedOwner, RemovedOwner and ChangedThreshold events): mainnet:verify compares the owners and the threshold with the plan’s when it is run, and the Safe’s holders watch their Safe. A freeze by the Safe could only be made slower by giving PAUSER or EMERGENCY to other keys than ADMIN’s, which v1 does not do.

One delay grew with the cancellers’ say: when a rogue canceller and a role that has to be revoked come together, the Safe needs 48 hours to remove the canceller and 48 more for the revocation.

What a canceller still cannot do: call anything, schedule anything, or stop its own removal. What a rogue canceller can do is delay, as above.

The deployer builds the manager, gives it its roles and gives up every role of its own in the launch (npm run mainnet:execute), and npm run mainnet:verify checks all of it on-chain against the reviewed plan: the manager’s code is the build’s byte for byte, the Safe is a genuine one with no modules or guard, every role’s members and delays, every function’s role, that the manager’s eight power-adding functions and revokeRole are mapped to GUARDED and revokeCanceller is not, and that no one else holds a role. Testnet vaults are owned by the deployer’s plain key.

Function Bounds
setFees(feeBps, minDepositFee, minReleaseFee) feeBps ≤ 100; each minimum ≤ 50 USDT
setDepositSurge(surgeFeeBps, capacity) The rising deposit fee: surgeFeeBps ≤ 300 (3 %); a meter of 0 (off) or 2 units to 10¹⁵
setDepositLimits(min, max) 0 < min ≤ max ≤ 10¹⁵ units
setReleaseLimits(capacity, refillPerSecond) capacity ≤ 10¹⁵; a full refill takes at least a day; the allowance earned so far is kept, capped at the new capacity
setPayoutDelay(seconds) 5 minutes to 24 hours; payouts already queued keep their time
cancelRelease(redeemId), cancelRefund(nonce) A payout still queued. Its allowance comes back, and it stays cancelled: the vault refuses to queue it again, whatever signatures come with it, until the owner reinstates it
reinstateRelease(redeemId), reinstateRefund(nonce) A cancelled payout. It can then be queued again with signatures of the current signers. A GOVERNOR call, so it waits 48 hours and the cancellers can cancel it
setSigners(address[5]) 5 distinct, non-zero addresses; effective immediately
setGuardian(address) Non-zero
unpause()
withdrawFees(to, units) units ≤ feesAccrued; never touches the backing
transferOwnership, then acceptOwnership Two steps; a pending transfer can be replaced or cancelled
pause() The guardian or the owner
renounceOwnership Always reverts

The owner cannot move backing. Only release and refund, with 3 signer signatures, reduce it.

KasPool: the admin key

Section titled “KasPool: the admin key”

The pool’s admin key can collect the protocol’s accrued fees (collect), resume a pool the guardian paused (unpause), and hand the admin key, the guardian key or both to other keys (set_keys). Only its signature opens the pool: the first deposit (init), and the first deposit into a pool that every provider has left, need it, so nobody can open or reopen the pool with dust. It cannot touch the reserves or any position. The circuit breaker’s values are fixed at genesis, so no key can change them. Before mainnet, the operator holds the admin key offline. Stolen, it can still hold every pool transaction up, withdrawals included, for a fee a spend (known limits).

Keeping the signer sets in step

Section titled “Keeping the signer sets in step”

The same five signers act on three contracts, and each contract has its own copy of the signer set:

Contract Holds Changed by
BridgeMint Hash of the five Schnorr keys 4 of the 5 current signers
Vault on Ethereum Five EVM addresses That vault’s owner
Vault on BNB Chain Five EVM addresses That vault’s owner

A signer rotation therefore takes three changes: one on Kaspa, signed by 4 signers, and one per vault, made by the owner.

No upgrades

Section titled “No upgrades”

Neither the vault nor the covenants have an upgrade path. The vault is not a proxy, and no covenant entry can change its own code. A code change means a new deployment: a new covenant ID and new vault addresses, always both. A vault is never replaced under a live kUSDT, and a kUSDT never over live vaults. Nothing in a burn names the vault that pays it and nothing in a deposit names the covenant that mints it, so a new vault under the old kUSDT would pay every earlier burn again, and a new kUSDT over the old vaults would mint every earlier deposit again (this page said “or” until round 12). Holders move to a new deployment by redeeming out of the old one. The pool is different: it is a covenant of its own that holds no bridge backing, and a new pool under the same kUSDT is an ordinary step.
