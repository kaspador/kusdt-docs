## What kUSDT is

kUSDT is a token on **Kaspa Layer 1** (L1). Each kUSDT is backed by one USDT locked in a vault contract on
Ethereum or on BNB Chain. kUSDT has 6 decimals, like USDT on Ethereum.

- On Kaspa, kUSDT is a **KCC-20 covenant token**. KCC-20 is the Kaspa convention for fungible tokens. A kUSDT
  balance is an ordinary Kaspa transaction output whose spending rules every Kaspa node checks. kUSDT uses the
  standard KCC-20 token code unchanged, so wallets that support KCC-20 can read it.
- On Ethereum and BNB Chain, one **vault** contract per chain holds the locked USDT.
- A **bridge** of 5 independent signers connects the two sides. Any 3 of them can approve a mint on Kaspa or a
  payout from a vault, and both sides limit how much can move per day.
- **KasPool**, a constant-product pool, lets anyone swap KAS for kUSDT and back. It also runs as covenants on
  Kaspa L1.
- **kUSDT Wallet**, a browser extension with KCC-20 support, signs everything on the Kaspa side. Its mainnet
  version is published right after the launch, and these docs will link it then.

## Why Kaspa L1

Everything that happens to kUSDT on the Kaspa side is a Kaspa L1 transaction. kUSDT does not use the Igra or
Kasplex layer-2 networks, their contracts or their bridges. Its rules run in Kaspa's own script engine, and its
transactions are ordered and confirmed like any KAS payment. [Kaspa L1, not a layer 2](/overview/why-kaspa-l1/)
explains the difference.

## How it works, in three steps

<div class="steps3">
  <div>
    <div class="n">Step 1</div>
    <div class="h">Deposit</div>
    <p>You lock USDT in the vault on Ethereum or BNB Chain and name a Kaspa address. The vault numbers the deposit and keeps a 0.50 % fee (at least 0.50 USDT).</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <div class="n">Step 2</div>
    <div class="h">Mint</div>
    <p>Once the deposit is final, at least 3 of the 5 signers sign it. The BridgeMint covenant on Kaspa checks the signatures and the limits, and creates your kUSDT.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <div class="n">Step 3</div>
    <div class="h">Use or redeem</div>
    <p>Send kUSDT, or swap it for KAS in KasPool. To redeem, you send it to a burn owner that names your EVM address. The signers approve, and the vault pays you USDT.</p>
  </div>
</div>

The full flows are in [Bridge USDT in](/getting-started/bridge-in/) and [Redeem kUSDT](/getting-started/redeem/).

## Key numbers

These are the mainnet defaults from the specification, with the values at the launch where they are lower.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Signers | 5; any 3 approve a mint or a payout; 4 unpause, change limits or replace signers on Kaspa |
| Bridge fee | 0.50 % each way; a deposit pays more, up to 2.50 %, while many deposits were made that day ([Fees](/protocol/fees/#the-rising-deposit-fee)) |
| Minimum fee | 0.50 USDT per deposit; 5 USDT per release on Ethereum, 0.50 USDT on BNB Chain |
| Deposit size | 10 to 100 000 USDT (8 000 at the mainnet launch) |
| Kaspa mint allowance | Twice the signers' daily limit, refills fully in 24 hours (100 000 USDT at the mainnet launch) |
| Vault release allowance | 250 000 USDT per vault, refills fully in 24 hours (50 000 at the mainnet launch) |
| Kaspa hard cap | 1 000 000 000 kUSDT; it can only go down |
| Pool fee | 0.50 % per swap (0.45 % to liquidity providers, 0.05 % to the protocol) |
