kUSDT is in development. It runs on testnet only (Kaspa testnet-10, Sepolia and BSC testnet); the testnet addresses are on Status. The contracts have not been audited. Do not send real funds to any address that claims to be kUSDT. Mainnet addresses will be published in these docs.

What kUSDT is

Section titled “What kUSDT is”

kUSDT is a token on Kaspa Layer 1 (L1). Each kUSDT is backed by one USDT locked in a vault contract on Ethereum or on BNB Chain. kUSDT has 6 decimals, like USDT on Ethereum.

  • On Kaspa, kUSDT is a KCC-20 covenant token. KCC-20 is the Kaspa convention for fungible tokens. A kUSDT balance is an ordinary Kaspa transaction output whose spending rules every Kaspa node checks. kUSDT uses the standard KCC-20 token code unchanged, so wallets that support KCC-20 can read it.
  • On Ethereum and BNB Chain, one vault contract per chain holds the locked USDT.
  • A bridge of 5 independent signers connects the two sides. Any 3 of them can approve a mint on Kaspa or a payout from a vault, and both sides limit how much can move per day.
  • KasPool, a constant-product pool, lets anyone swap KAS for kUSDT and back. It also runs as covenants on Kaspa L1.
  • kUSDT Wallet, a browser extension with KCC-20 support, has a testnet build to download. It is not in any store.

Why Kaspa L1

Section titled “Why Kaspa L1”

Everything that happens to kUSDT on the Kaspa side is a Kaspa L1 transaction. kUSDT does not use the Igra or Kasplex layer-2 networks, their contracts or their bridges. Its rules run in Kaspa’s own script engine, and its transactions are ordered and confirmed like any KAS payment. Kaspa L1, not a layer 2 explains the difference.

How it works, in three steps

Section titled “How it works, in three steps”
Step 1
Deposit

You lock USDT in the vault on Ethereum or BNB Chain and name a Kaspa address. The vault numbers the deposit and keeps a 0.20 % fee (at least 0.50 USDT).

Step 2
Mint

Once the deposit is final, at least 3 of the 5 signers sign it. The BridgeMint covenant on Kaspa checks the signatures and the limits, and creates your kUSDT.

Step 3
Use or redeem

Send kUSDT, or swap it for KAS in KasPool. To redeem, you send it to a burn owner that names your EVM address. The signers approve, and the vault pays you USDT.

The full flows are in Bridge USDT in and Redeem kUSDT.

Key numbers

Section titled “Key numbers”

These are the mainnet defaults from the specification. Testnet uses the same rules.

Item Value
Signers 5; any 3 approve a mint or a payout; 4 unpause, change limits or replace signers on Kaspa
Bridge fee 0.20 % each way
Minimum fee 0.50 USDT per deposit; 5 USDT per release on Ethereum, 0.50 USDT on BNB Chain
Deposit size 10 to 100 000 USDT
Kaspa mint allowance 500 000 USDT, refills fully in 24 hours
Vault release allowance 250 000 USDT per vault, refills fully in 24 hours
Kaspa hard cap 1 000 000 000 kUSDT; it can only go down
Pool fee 0.30 % per swap (0.25 % to liquidity providers, 0.05 % to the protocol)
