KasPool is a KAS⇄kUSDT pool on Kaspa L1. It holds a reserve of KAS and a reserve of kUSDT and prices every swap
by the *constant-product* rule: after a swap, the product of the two reserves may not shrink. Each swap is a
single Kaspa transaction.

:::note[Testnet only]
The KasPool covenants are tested in Kaspa's script engine and run on testnet (TN10). The testnet pool is being
re-created with the circuit breaker; its covenant ID is **to be published**. The web app is not public yet, and
nothing is on mainnet.
:::

:::tip[In the web app]
The [web app](/getting-started/web-app/)'s Swap page quotes with the same core the covenant checks against, and
shows the 0.30 % fee, the price impact and your slippage setting. The wallet then builds and signs the swap.
:::

## How a swap works

1. **Get a quote.** Your app reads the pool's current state: the KAS reserve *X* and the kUSDT reserve *Y*.
2. **Build the transaction.** The app builds a transaction that spends the pool UTXO, the pool's kUSDT reserve
   and your own coins, and names the **exact** amount you receive.
3. **Sign and submit.** Your wallet signs your inputs. The pool covenant accepts the transaction only if the
   reserves after the swap satisfy the constant-product rule with the fee applied.

## Price and fee

Every swap pays **0.30 %** of its input: 0.25 % stays in the pool for liquidity providers, 0.05 % accrues to
the protocol. The best amount a swap can return is:

```text
effective = floor(input × 9 970 / 10 000)
output    = floor(reserve_out × effective / (reserve_in + effective))
```

For a buy, the input is KAS, `reserve_in` is *X* and `reserve_out` is *Y*. For a sell, the input is kUSDT,
`reserve_in` is *Y* and `reserve_out` is *X*. Asking for one base unit more than this quote is rejected.

| Pool | You send | You receive (quote) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 000 KAS and 1 000 kUSDT | 100 KAS | about 9.87 kUSDT |

A round trip (KAS to kUSDT and back) returns a little less than you started with, about the 0.6 % paid in
fees. You also pay the normal Kaspa network fee in KAS.

## No sandwiching, no slippage after signing

A swap transaction spends the exact pool UTXO it was built on and names the exact amount you receive. Two
things follow:

- **Nobody can worsen your price after you sign.** If another swap lands first, it spends that pool UTXO.
  Your transaction then becomes invalid, rather than executing at a worse price.
- **You may need to retry.** In a busy moment your app may have to re-quote and re-sign against the new pool
  state.
- **The wallet asks before a worse price.** It goes ahead on its own only if the new quote is at least as good as
  the one you confirmed. Your slippage minimum stays the hard floor. (This is being built in the wallet now.)

## Selling kUSDT

A sell is the mirror image. Your kUSDT state is an input, any remaining kUSDT returns to you as change, and the
KAS goes to an output you choose.

### The sell limit

To bound a dump of forged kUSDT, sells may take at most 10 % of the pool's KAS reserve per hour (the
[circuit breaker](/contracts/kaspool/#the-circuit-breaker)). Buys are not limited. If your sell is over what is
left, the app tells you that the pool lets at most so much KAS out until about a given time. Sell less, or wait
for the next window.

## When the pool is paused

The bridge's guardian pauses the pool on any alarm, with the vaults and the minter. While it is paused:

| Stops | Keeps working |
|---|---|
| Buys, sells and adding liquidity | Removing liquidity: you can always withdraw your Position |

Only the pool admin resumes it.

## Providing liquidity

Anyone can add KAS and kUSDT in the current ratio and receive a **Position**: a covenant UTXO that records
liquidity shares and the key that owns them. Burning the Position returns at most its share of each reserve.
The owner can transfer a Position to another key. See [KasPool](/contracts/kaspool/) for the exact rules.
