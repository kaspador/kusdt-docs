Threat model

This page goes through each party that could be compromised and states what follows. Numbers use the mainnet defaults from Limits: a Kaspa mint allowance of 500 000 USDT and a release allowance of 250 000 USDT per vault, each refilling fully in 24 hours. Because an allowance is a bucket that can be emptied and then refills, up to twice its ceiling can move in any 24-hour window.

Summary

Section titled “Summary”
Compromised Kaspa side EVM side (each vault)
1 or 2 signers Nothing Nothing
3 signers (a bad quorum) Mint unbacked kUSDT within the allowance, while not paused Pay out without burns, within the release allowance, while not paused
4 or 5 signers Raise the mint allowance, so only the remaining hard cap bounds minting; unpause; replace the signers and the guardian Same as 3: the vault’s allowance and signer set belong to the owner
Guardian key Pause the minter and the pool Pause the vault
Coordinator Stall Stall
Monitor Pause, or fail to pause Pause, or fail to pause
Vault owner Nothing After the 48-hour timelock: replace the signers and raise limits within the hard bounds
Pool admin Take the accrued protocol fees, unpause the pool, hand the keys over Nothing

Attack vectors

Section titled “Attack vectors”

A review of every attack we know of, as of 4 November 2026. Status means:

  • tested: an attack run or a test shows the defence works;
  • bounded: the attack works, but a limit caps what it can take;
  • by design: the protocol’s shape rules it out;
  • open: not solved yet;
  • fixed: found and fixed, with the date.

Bridge

Section titled “Bridge”
Attack What stops it Status
3 of 5 signer keys mint unbacked kUSDT The mint allowance: 500 000 at once, at most 1 000 000 in any 24 hours. The monitor pauses within seconds Tested: the 400 000 forged mint in local:e2e
3 of 5 signer keys pay out without burns Each vault’s release allowance: 250 000 at once. Up to about 250 000 per vault can leave before the pause Bounded
4 of 5 signer keys They can raise the Kaspa limits and unpause the minter. The vaults’ limits and signers belong to the owner (a Safe behind a 48-hour timelock on mainnet) Bounded on the vaults; the monitor is the defence on Kaspa
A stolen guardian key It can only pause: griefing By design
A lying coordinator or API It can stall or censor, nothing more. Wallets check token states against their own node and pin the deployment; signers re-check everything By design
Fake deposits or burns fed to a signer through its RPC or node It takes 3 operators’ nodes. Today all 5 testnet signers run on one machine Open until there are 5 independent operators
Reorgs Finality on both sides; the follower goes back to the fork point if Kaspa’s chain moves anyway Fixed 4 November 2026 (the follower rewind)
Replay across chains, vaults or deployments The EIP-712 domain, the covenant ID in the digests, each redeemId paid once Tested
Decoy outputs fooling the followers Successors come only from covenant-bound authorized outputs Fixed in the security review, with tests
Tether blacklists a recipient or a vault Nothing is paid to a blocked address; a blocked vault pauses everything Fixed 4 November 2026 (Blocked recipients)
Big legitimate flows that use up the allowances Liveness only: the bridge waits for the refill. It costs the attacker 0.2 % each way Bounded

Swap

Section titled “Swap”
Attack What stops it Status
Forged kUSDT dumped into the pool The biggest one. A forged mint and a sell can land in the same block, so no monitor can stop it. The pool’s circuit breaker stops it on-chain: sells take at most 10 % of the KAS reserve per hour (KasPool). The monitor then pauses the pool with everything else: swaps and new liquidity stop, withdrawals stay open, and only the pool admin resumes Fixed 4 November 2026. Tested: in local:e2e, 400 000 forged kUSDT get under 10 % of the pool’s KAS
Sandwiching A swap spends the exact pool state it was quoted on, so a front-run makes it fail, never fill worse. But the wallet used to re-quote by itself within your slippage, so a bot could take up to that slippage. Now the wallet asks before a worse price: it goes ahead on its own only when a new quote is at least as good as the one you confirmed By design on-chain; the wallet fix is being built
Inflating the first depositor’s shares 1 000 shares are locked at init and can never be withdrawn By design
Rounding and overflow Exact 128-bit products and bounds on every input Tested; needs the audit
Swap spam Griefing only: each swap pays the network fee and 0.30 % Bounded
Price manipulation Nothing reads the pool price as an oracle By design
A stolen pool admin key It can take the accrued fees, unpause and hand the keys over. It cannot touch the reserves or a position Bounded

Users and apps

Section titled “Users and apps”
Attack What stops it Status
Phishing: a fake app, extension or token The wallet pins the covenant ID and the vaults. Check the covenant ID By design
Malicious sites and clipboard swaps The wallet’s approval window shows the exact address. It is the last defence By design; it relies on the user reading it
The extension’s supply chain (npm dependencies) A lockfile audit and reproducible builds Open
Mistakes A burn to a wrong address cannot be undone. The wallet refuses the burns it knows can’t be paid By design

Before mainnet, in order

Section titled “Before mainnet, in order”
  1. The pool’s circuit breaker. Done.
  2. No silent worse re-quotes in the wallet. Being built.
  3. At least 2 independent monitors, with external alerts on /health.
  4. Five independent signer operators.
  5. External audits of the covenants, their compiler and the vault, and allowances sized to what can be lost. With 3 stolen keys, today’s limits risk about 500 000 USDT from the vaults, plus at most about 10 % of the pool’s KAS.

See also the mainnet checklist.

The root cause

Section titled “The root cause”

Every serious attack needs 3 signer keys stolen together, or a bug in a covenant or its compiler. The pool cannot tell forged kUSDT from real: it is the same token, with the same covenant ID. The breaker bounds the damage. The real defence is keeping 3 keys from ever being stolen together.

Signers

Section titled “Signers”

One or two signer keys

Section titled “One or two signer keys”

Every mint, release and refund needs 3 distinct signers. One or two keys can sign nothing that a contract accepts (INV-4).

A bad quorum: three signer keys

Section titled “A bad quorum: three signer keys”
Side Can Cannot
Kaspa Mint kUSDT that no deposit backs, up to the mint allowance: 500 000 USDT at once, at most 1 000 000 USDT in any 24 hours Exceed the allowance or the hard cap; unpause the minter; change the limits, the signers or the guardian (all of these need 4)
Each vault Sign releases and refunds that no burn or skip justifies, up to the release allowance: 250 000 USDT at once, at most 500 000 USDT in any 24 hours Exceed the allowance; change any vault parameter; unpause a paused vault

Unbacked mints or releases make kUSDT in circulation exceed the backing. The monitor checks this (INV-1) and the guardian pauses the vaults and the minter. A bad quorum of 3 cannot undo that pause on either side: unpausing BridgeMint takes 4 signers, and unpausing a vault takes the owner. The owner then replaces the vault signers with setSigners.

Unbacked kUSDT also harms holders and the pool. Sold into KasPool, it takes KAS from the pool’s liquidity providers. The pool’s circuit breaker lets sells take at most 10 % of the KAS reserve per hour, and the guardian then pauses the pool (see KasPool).

Four or five signer keys

Section titled “Four or five signer keys”

With 4 keys the Kaspa side loses its limits, because 4 signers set them. set_limits can raise the ceiling and the refill rate, so minting is bounded only by the remaining hard cap (remaining, 1 billion USDT at launch). unpause can undo the guardian’s pause of the minter, and set_signers can replace the signer set and the guardian, which takes over BridgeMint for good. On the Kaspa side, the INV-1 monitor is then the defence.

The vaults are unaffected beyond the 3-key case. Their release allowance and signer sets are set only by the vault owner, so USDT leaves each vault no faster than its release allowance, and the guardian pauses the vaults the moment INV-1 fails. Only the owner can unpause them.

Guardian

Section titled “Guardian”

The guardian key can pause BridgeMint, both vaults and the pool, and do nothing else. A stolen guardian key can stop the bridge and the pool:

Side Who resumes Who replaces the guardian
Kaspa 4 signers (unpause) 4 signers (set_signers)
Vault The owner (unpause) The owner (setGuardian); on mainnet through the 48-hour timelock
Pool The pool admin (unpause) The pool admin (set_keys)

kUSDT transfers on Kaspa are not affected by any pause. A paused pool still lets liquidity providers withdraw.

Coordinator

Section titled “Coordinator”

The coordinator holds no key that moves funds. It can:

  • stall the bridge, by not collecting or not submitting signatures;
  • report false status through its API.

It cannot redirect funds: every signature binds the exact recipient and amount, and the contracts check them. Anyone holding valid signatures can submit them without the coordinator. Every important API answer can be checked against a node.

Monitor

Section titled “Monitor”

The monitor reads every chain from its own nodes and holds the guardian keys. On any alarm it pauses both vaults, the minter and the pool; it never unpauses. A compromised monitor can pause (as above) or fail to pause when something breaks. Without a pause, the allowances still cap what 3 bad signers can move.

Evidence: a bad quorum in the local run

Section titled “Evidence: a bad quorum in the local run”

The local end-to-end run (npm run local:e2e, passed on 3 November 2026) plays the 3-key attack:

Step Result
Before the attack The monitor agrees with the coordinator: minted 299, released 50, owed 249 ≤ backing 249 (USDT), no alarms
Three stolen signer keys mint 1 000 000 kUSDT Refused by the mint allowance
They mint 400 000 kUSDT with no deposit Accepted by the covenant, which cannot see the EVM chains
At once, they sell all 400 000 into the pool The circuit breaker refuses the whole sell. The most it lets out is under 10 % of the pool’s KAS, chained on the forged mint
The monitor Raises inv1; within seconds, both vaults, the minter and the pool are paused
The coordinator Follows the forged mint and the pause
A new deposit Refused by the paused vault

The forged kUSDT stays on Kaspa, where transfers are not paused. The pause keeps it from being released for USDT or sold into the pool.

Vault owner

Section titled “Vault owner”

The owner is a Safe behind a 48-hour timelock on mainnet. A compromised owner can, after the timelock:

  • replace a vault’s signers with its own keys (setSigners);
  • raise the release allowance to the hard bounds: a capacity of up to 1 billion USDT, refilling fully in no less than a day;
  • set fees up to 1 % and minimum fees up to 50 USDT;
  • withdraw accrued fees.

It cannot withdraw backing directly, and every change becomes public 48 hours before it takes effect. The owner is the one party whose compromise is not capped by the release allowance, which is why it sits behind a multisig and a timelock.

Chains and nodes

Section titled “Chains and nodes”
Threat Mitigation
A reorg on the source chain after a mint or release Act only on final deposits and burns (see Finality)
Kaspa’s selected chain moves off a block a follower already read The follower reads again from the fork point; a transaction it already followed spends a state it no longer holds, so it is ignored (see Following covenant states)
A load-balanced EVM RPC answers from a node behind the head it reported Reads stay evmHeadLag blocks (2 by default) below the head
A signer’s own node lies to it Each signer runs its own nodes; a lie fools one signer, and a quorum needs 3
Replay across chains, vaults or deployments Digests name the covenant ID; EIP-712 names the chain ID and the vault
Tether blacklists a release or refund recipient Signers refuse to sign it (USDT would pay it and freeze it there); the coordinator holds it as blocked and pays it once unblocked
Tether blacklists a vault The vault can pay nothing out. The monitor raises vault-blocked and the guardian pauses everything
