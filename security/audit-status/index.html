Audit status

Not audited

No part of kUSDT has been audited. Not the Kaspa covenants, not the vault, not the transaction builders. Do not use kUSDT with funds you cannot afford to lose.

What exists today

Section titled “What exists today”
Part Evidence
BridgeMint Tests in Kaspa’s script engine, including attacks; every test transaction checked for relay standardness (175 Rust tests in all, with the pool’s and the builders’)
kUSDT token family The KCC20 template matches the standard byte for byte; the checked-in artifact matches the source
KasPool Tests in Kaspa’s script engine, including theft attempts through a Position and through the admin path, the circuit breaker and the pause
KusdtVault 209 Hardhat tests, including randomized invariant runs and non-standard tokens; reviewed line by line by the project owner against the specification
Services, deployer, clients Unit tests: services 672 (one of them an expected failure), deployer and test tools 118, protocol 57, Kaspa client 60, web app 249, wallet 227
End to end (Kaspa) Every Kaspa-side transaction, bridge and pool, accepted by a kaspad v2.1.0 simnet node
End to end (whole bridge) local:e2e with a forged-mint attack caught by the monitor and its dump into the pool capped by the circuit breaker; local:load with concurrent deposits, unpayable burns, a refund, a burn and a refund held for a blocked address and paid once unblocked, and a blocked vault paused
Mainnet forks fork:rehearsal: the vault against the real USDT and BSC-USD, including USDT’s blacklist and BSC-USD’s lack of one

Tests show that the code does what its authors intended in the cases they thought of. They are not an audit.

Internal security review (4 November 2026)

Section titled “Internal security review (4 November 2026)”

An internal review of the money paths, made by the project’s own tooling, not an external audit. It found the vault, the signers’ checks and the covenants sound, and found problems in the off-chain followers and services, all fixed:

Severity Finding Fix
High Followers adopted decoy outputs: plain outputs paying to a successor’s script Successors come only from the input’s covenant-bound authorized outputs; a new minter-lost alarm. Regression tests in decoys.test.ts, decoy_outputs.rs and local:load
High One unpayable burn could block every release Per-burn backoff and the unpayable status
Medium A stray pool-owned payer bricked the pool follower Fixed in the pool follower
Medium No refund path existed /sign/refund on each signer, which follows the minter itself, and a coordinator refund loop
Low Monitor RPC-error handling; the signer bearer token; health on Kaspa lag Errors handled; the bearer is mandatory and compared in constant time; /health answers 503 while Kaspa lags

Since decided: a recipient blocked by USDT is never paid. Wallets ask before a burn, signers refuse, and a burn that lands anyway is held until the address is unblocked. See Blocked recipients.

External review (5 November 2026)

Section titled “External review (5 November 2026)”

An outside reviewer sent twelve questions and findings. Each is answered below; the code changes are in the mainnet release candidates since commit 1e3f969 (the current one is mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.25). This is still not an audit.

# Finding Response
1 3 of 5 signers can release USDT without a burn, up to 250 000 per vault at once True, and the largest risk in v1. Bounded by each vault’s release allowance (under 750 000 per vault in any 24 hours, see Limits). The monitor alarms on a release with no burn at once and pauses. Signers can now keep their keys in an HSM or KMS (Operations), which stops a key being copied, not a signer host someone took over from having any mint, governance action, release or refund signed: the HSM is given only a 32-byte digest (run 21, R21-CRYPTO-6), and checking what it signs would need the message’s fields. The zero-knowledge upgrade covers deposits only; releases stay federated until the EVM side can verify Kaspa (Known limits)
2 The vault owner is de facto control, and nobody can cancel a bad proposal The owner is now an OpenZeppelin AccessManager whose admin is the Safe: 48-hour delays, cancellers who can cancel any queued vault change (and, since rc.16, the Safe’s power-adding changes to the manager itself), and a monitor that alarms the moment a change is queued and can cancel it (Governance)
3 Three signer sets can drift apart A rotation runbook with tools (rotate-plan, govern-sign, govern-submit) and a local rehearsal; the monitor accepts only announced sets (Rotating a signer)
4 The testnet runs all signers on one host True; mainnet needs five independent operators, each with its own node and RPCs. The mainnet packages are built for that, and five operators are lined up
5 A burn is final after about 100 seconds, before a payout of up to 250 000 Larger burns now wait longer: 10 minutes above 10 000 kUSDT, an hour above 100 000 (Finality)
6 Forged kUSDT can be dumped into other pools True; kUSDT transfers cannot be paused. The monitor now serves a fixed-shape signal for integrators, GET /api/v1/signal (The monitor)
7 One monitor, one guardian; recovery takes 48 hours Two monitors may share the guardian key (it can only pause). The vault’s setGuardian and unpause now wait about 6 hours, not 48 (A stolen guardian key)
8 No audit; the source is not public No auditor is booked yet. The repository stays private: auditors and operators get access to it, and the covenants’ compiled artifacts and the deployment record let anyone check the pinned covenant IDs against what is on-chain (Verify the template)
9 One token, two vaults Rebalancing through the bridge’s own paths (Rebalancing the vaults)
10 Small redemptions lose KAS and can be lost entirely True of hand-built transactions; the wallet and the web app refuse a burn at or below the fee and warn above 20 %
11 Round trips can use up the pool’s sell room Fixed: the circuit breaker counts the net flow (KasPool)
12 A burn to a blacklisted address is held indefinitely Intended, decided on 4 November 2026: a redirect power would be a new theft path and would help a blocked party evade the block. The legal review should confirm it

Outside source review of the services (5 November 2026)

Section titled “Outside source review of the services (5 November 2026)”

A second outside review read the off-chain services and filed three findings. None touches the covenants or the vault. All three were confirmed in the code and fixed the same day, in commit 4a037d0:

# Finding Fix
1 The RPCs had to agree on only some fields of a deposit, not its sender, which a refund pays Every field of a deposit, and of every release and refund, must agree across a service’s RPCs
2 Redemption reports were first-writer-wins and unverified: a wrong first report could strand someone’s burn Claims are checked against the coordinator’s node, which decides; a client’s transaction can no longer edit the token index (Reporting a burn)
3 The monitor failed open: one unreadable RPC stopped the whole check, the pause included Each chain is checked on its own, INV-1 is never judged on partial backing, and a chain unreadable for 10 minutes pauses everything (The monitor)

Checking finding 1 turned up the same gap in the releases and refunds the monitor reads (only their amounts were compared); it is fixed with it.

Section titled “Third outside review (6 November 2026)”

# Severity as reported Finding Response
1 High set_signers (4 of 5) replaces the signer set and the minter’s guardian together, so the quorum the pause polices can remove it Accepted by the project owner: the covenant does not change. The same 4 keys can already unpause and raise the limits, so this adds little to what 4 keys can do. Mint retries (7 November 2026)

Section titled “Mint retries (7 November 2026)”

An outside reviewer reported that a failed mint could leave the signers and the coordinator on different batches for good, and the public testnet stopped minting the same day for that reason. It touches neither the covenants nor the vault, and is fixed in commit e8e8d71, in the release candidates since mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.9.

Severity Finding Fix
High (liveness) A signer signs one batch per starting nonce and refuses any other. If the first request timed out after the signers had recorded it, and another deposit arrived, every retry asked for a larger batch and was refused, and that chain’s failure kept the other chain from minting The coordinator saves the batch before it asks and asks for exactly it until the minter moves past it. If the signers say they signed another one, it tries the shorter lengths, which is all two batches at one nonce can differ in. Each chain gets its own turn, and a mint request waits 120 seconds for the signers

On the testnet, three BNB testnet deposits went final within ten seconds, and the first request (one deposit) took the signers 35 to 95 seconds over a slow RPC. The fix recovered it without touching the signers’ records. The same incident showed a second limit: a signer or monitor that restarts reads every deposit again from the vault’s first block, so its RPCs must keep logs that far back. Some free RPCs keep only a few days.

Since mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.10:

  • Limits that fit together. Checking the launch values against the signers’ agreed daily limit (25 000 kUSDT) showed that a deposit or a burn larger than a signer’s limit, or a batch of deposits adding up to more, would be accepted by the vaults and then never signed. The deployer now refuses vault limits that don’t fit the signers’ (Limits), and the coordinator splits a batch that every signer finds over its limit.
  • Restarts read only new blocks. Each signer and monitor keeps the deposits it read, so a restart no longer needs RPCs with the whole history.
  • A mint the monitor can’t check fails closed: after failClosedMs it is the unverifiable alarm, and everything pauses, as for a chain it can’t read.

An internal review of these changes found a way the batch split could stall a chain, and a way the saved deposits could be written mid-scan. Both are fixed in the candidate. It is not an outside review.

Fifth review (8 November 2026)

Section titled “Fifth review (8 November 2026)”

Eight findings, each checked against the code. All were real, and all are fixed in mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.11. The first changes the vault contract, and no outside review has seen that change. This is still not an audit.

# Severity as reported Finding Fix
1 P1 The allowance is drawn when a payout is queued, and nothing capped the queue: payouts left waiting for days could all be paid in one block The queue holds at most one allowance (Limits)
2 P1 A signer’s record and charge changed in memory before the save. After a failed save, a retry found the record and signed with nothing on disk, and a restart forgot both Recorded, charged and on disk, or none of it. Saves now reach the disk before the signature is given
3 P1 A follower more than 100 000 DAA behind threw away what it had just fetched, and stalled for good What it holds ages by its own position, not the network’s
4 P1 During a Kaspa catch-up, a queued payout whose status could not be read raised nothing, so the pause could be missed It counts as still queued, and fails closed
5 P2 A transaction’s payload named the recipient, unchecked against what the burn commits to. A wrong one became final, could never be paid, and a correct report came too late A recipient or chain the burn doesn’t commit to is not recorded. Checking it found that a burn naming a chain the bridge doesn’t have could block every other redemption: also fixed
6 P2 One malformed signature threw away the whole round, so one bad signer could stop the bridge It costs only that signer, and each signer counts once
7 P2 Unused fields a client sent in a token state were stored, past the limits on claims Only the state’s own fields are kept, each checked. Checking it found that another spelling of a burn’s outpoint (an uppercase id, or an index past 2³²) could hold a real burn as invalid: an outpoint now has one spelling
8 P2 An empty bearer file turned off a signer’s login check An empty token refuses to start

Attack testing (8 November 2026)

Section titled “Attack testing (8 November 2026)”

After the fifth review, each part of the system was attacked in turn, and every claimed bug had to be proven by a failing test: the vault, the Kaspa covenants, the signer, the coordinator, the monitor, and the wallet, web app and deployer. The real services were also run as separate processes and killed outright at bad moments, with RPCs that stalled, failed and lied. This is internal testing: it is not an audit and no outside party has checked it. All of it is in mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.12. The covenants, the vault and the services all changed, and no outside review has seen those changes.

The most serious findings, all fixed:

Severity Finding Fix
High A Kaspa transaction id does not cover its signature scripts, so a miner could publish a rewritten body. A real governance transaction rewritten as a skip could make a signer sign a refund of a deposit that was minted A follower accepts a body only if its program hashes to the spent script and its signatures verify against the held signer set
High The pool’s circuit breaker read a clock with no lower bound, so idle windows could be stacked into one dump. A first redesign could still be drained by repeated buys and sells The clock is now the age of the pool’s UTXO, which consensus proves. The reference rises only with that age, and used room is never forgiven
High One crafted Kaspa transaction could make the coordinator and the monitor lose the rest of a scanned batch A bad state decodes to nothing, and each transaction is handled on its own
High One frozen or hung RPC could leave the monitor blind while it reported healthy, or delay the pause past the payout wait A frozen head and sustained Kaspa lag fail closed, every RPC call has a hard deadline, and pauses run at the same time and are replaced if stuck
High A deposit a lagging RPC had not indexed was skipped for good and stalled its chain The coordinator reads a tail again, needs its RPCs to agree, and checks for gaps in the deposit nonces
High verify --plan never compared the signers with the reviewed plan It compares them on the deployment, the minter and both vaults
Medium Three keys could void a signed governance action through set_value set_value needs 4 of 5

Since then (9 and 10 November 2026): three more rounds, the last over the whole codebase by threat. No critical finding. 15 high: 13 are fixed, and 2 are open and listed under Known limits (one party can use up the signers’ daily limit, since addressed for deposits by a fee that rises with use, see Round 10 and after; USDT sent straight to a vault is lost). The fixes of the last round were themselves attacked before the release candidate, three times for the wallet, and what that found is fixed. Two rounds in a row with no critical or high finding, the rule before mainnet, have not been reached.

Smaller fixes: a watcher could garble a pending governance transaction; one chain’s RPC outage stopped the other’s work; burns could be given up on; funding coins could leak; the wallet accepted a mistyped address and trusted the coordinator for the fee; the vault’s allowance now counts the whole amount.

What held up: random-sequence testing of the vault found no bug in it. Under real kills, every deposit was minted exactly once, every burn was paid once, and the books agreed to the unit. Three stolen keys queueing a payout while the monitor was down were stopped when it came back inside the wait.

What is still open. The final design of the pool’s circuit breaker passed its tests and a randomized test through the real script engine, but the last independent attempt to break it did not complete, so it has had no independent adversarial review. It should have a human review before the pool holds real value. A deep reorg of more than 20 DAA does not roll back the coordinator’s view of the pool or positions; that surfaces as an alarm and fails closed. Attackers working from the code can miss things: this testing lowers the risk, it does not end it.

Round 10 (10 November 2026)

Section titled “Round 10 (10 November 2026)”

A tenth internal round, over the whole codebase by threat, on mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.15. Eight reviewers were set to it and seven ran. The one for the covenants did not run, so the covenants were not reviewed in this round. It is internal testing, not an audit.

No critical finding. Three areas had no high finding either: the vault’s owner and the launch (8 low), double minting and replay (3 low), and forged minting (1 medium, 2 low). The six high findings were in the services (3), the monitor’s judging of payouts (1), the coordinator’s payout queue (1) and the web app (1). All six are fixed, each with tests:

# Where Finding Fix
1 Monitor Any kUSDT output owned by a script hash was recorded as a possible burn, for good. Someone moving a few units through their own script could grow the monitor’s state, for network fees alone, until it could no longer be saved and the monitor died in every round A record lasts only while its output is unspent. A real burn can never be spent, so real burns are kept
2 Monitor and services One RPC answer, small on the wire and huge once unpacked, ended the process. A monitor that died in every round started with fresh clocks each time, so it never failed closed: nothing was judged or paused RPC answers are asked for uncompressed, refused if compressed, and cut off at 16 MB. A monitor that starts three times over 5 minutes without finishing a round pauses everything and raises crash-loop (The monitor)
3 Coordinator API Request bodies that were opened and never finished could hold more memory than the coordinator has, so it was killed and restarted for as long as the sender liked Bodies are limited in size, in number and per client, and must arrive within 10 seconds
4 Monitor With three stolen signer keys: a refund of a deposit that Kaspa never skipped could stay unjudged until it was paid, if one of the monitor’s Kaspa rounds failed every two minutes (one RPC naming a block time ahead of the others was enough to fail a round) A failed round no longer restarts the refund’s grace; such a refund is judged in the second up-to-date round; any queued payout still unjudged at two thirds of its wait fails closed
5 Coordinator Burns that could never be paid (empty ones, borrowable states moved again and again, dust below the fee) were recorded and took their turn ahead of real burns. A few a second were enough to hold payouts back Only plain burns above zero are recorded, and burns below the fee are set aside all at once
6 Web app A WalletConnect session written into the page’s storage could come to count as the visitor’s own wallet, and the bridge-back form then filled in its address as theirs A stored session never counts as your own address. Only a wallet that approved the connection during this visit does; otherwise you connect again or type the address

Still open from this round: the coordinator keeps every record and its kUSDT index in memory, and the monitor keeps real burns for good. Both can be grown on purpose, at a real cost in KAS, until the service stops. It is a stall, with no funds at risk, and it is to be fixed before mainnet (Known limits).

Two rounds in a row with no critical or high finding, the rule before mainnet, have not been reached. This round found six high, so the count starts again. Its fixes, the changed vault, the changed minter and the new pool covenant have had no round of their own, and no outside review has seen them.

An operator’s review of rc.15 (10 November 2026)

Section titled “An operator’s review of rc.15 (10 November 2026)”

One of the bridge’s operators reviewed mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.15 with tests of their own. It is a review from outside the team that writes the code, not an audit.

Its verdict: not ready for mainnet yet. In the contracts it found no way to steal money, to mint kUSDT without three signers, to mint twice for one deposit, to be paid twice for one burn, or to take USDT out of a vault without three signers or the vault’s owner. It did not test the signer, the coordinator and the monitor programs, nor the wallet and the site, and it names the three services as its largest gap.

It listed ten things to fix or decide before mainnet, A to J. This is what was done about each:

Point What was done
A The pool’s first deposit asks for no signature, so anyone holding a little kUSDT could open the pool with dust and keep its sell brake almost shut Fixed in the new pool covenant: the first deposit, and a deposit into an emptied pool, need the pool admin’s signature
B The Safe is the whole owner: whoever controls it could empty a vault about 48 hours after their first move, and neither a canceller nor a pause stopped that. One lost key on the Safe that needs all its owners would lock that vault’s owner for good Partly. The cancellers can now cancel the Safe’s power-adding calls, so a stolen Safe needs 48 hours from the cancellers’ last cancel, and 96 only while they cancel every try until they are removed (Governance; the first wording, “about 96 hours”, was corrected in round 12). Since round 11 they can also cancel the removal of the Safe’s own roles, so a pause that outlasts the Safe’s keys takes as long. The pause itself does not: a holder of the Safe’s keys pauses at once and cancels every unpause while it holds them. The Ethereum Safe goes from 3 of 3 to 2 of 3, as on BNB Chain (the owner’s decision of 13 November; Daybreak [1]), and that change is the transaction that proves every key works: all three current owners approve changeThreshold(2), Safe transaction hash 0xfec31923ea32d148c976a441da800e12b1bf5d2864ed748643872960675be3fa at nonce 0, two signing it in the Safe app and the third executing it from their own account. Still open, both on the launch list: that transaction (until it is executed, the Ethereum Safe needs all three owners, and one lost key would lock that vault’s owner for good), and writing down who holds each Safe key
C Launch day: while a vault is launched, the launching key alone has full power over it for a short time. The review planted six kinds of back door in that window, and each worked afterwards Each of the six is now planted in a test, and the launch check (mainnet:verify) fails on it. Still to do on launch day: someone other than the deployer reads both chains before any money goes in (launch list)
D Cancelling a bad payout did not kill it: anyone could queue it again with the same three signatures until the signers were replaced, and the runbook gave “cancel” alone as the remedy Fixed in the vault: a cancelled payout stays cancelled until the owner reinstates it (KusdtVault). The runbook lines are corrected
E The wording “25 000 USDT of releases per vault per day” is not a hard daily cap: after a quiet time about twice that left a vault in 24 hours in the review’s test The wording is corrected on the Limits page and on the launch list. The numbers are unchanged
F A team action on the minter can be sent again with a smaller age. Delay only: built-up mint allowance is thrown away Fixed in the minter: the signers of a team action now sign the age it claims, as a mint’s signers do, so a copy with another age fails the signature check. The cost is that a signed age is also a wait when a mint lands first, so the signing tools show the age, sign a rotation, a recovery proposal and a veto for age 0 (which never waits), and take --age 0 for any other action. What remains is under Known limits
G Four of the five signers could set limits so large that the minter stopped for good Fixed in the minter: set_limits refuses values above the lifetime cap
H Anyone could keep the pool’s sell brake shut. Sells are delayed, no money is lost Addressed in the new pool covenant: the least claim while more than half of the cap is in use, the public tick, and the keeper. What is left is under Known limits

Are the Ethereum and BNB Chain vaults safe?

Section titled “Are the Ethereum and BNB Chain vaults safe?”

As far as testing can show, and not audited yet.

Tested.

  • 209 vault tests: replay, signature order and malleability, reentrancy, fee-on-transfer tokens, backing never overdrawn, and the rate limits.
  • Fork rehearsals on the real USDT and BSC-USD, including the blacklist checks.
  • The internal security review found the vault sound.

By design.

  • Backing leaves only through a release or a refund signed by 3 of 5, within the release allowance.
  • The owner cannot take backing, only accrued fees. The guardian can only pause.
  • No proxy and no upgrade key, so nobody can swap the code. The flip side: a bug cannot be patched. It means a new deployment (new vaults and a new kUSDT covenant together, never a new vault under the old kUSDT: a burn names no vault, so the new one would pay every earlier burn again), and migrating through redemptions.

Not yet.

  • No external audit.
  • On testnet the owner is the deployer’s plain key. On mainnet the deploy script refuses any owner that is not a contract (an AccessManager whose admin is the Safe).

See the attack vectors for the whole bridge and the pool.
