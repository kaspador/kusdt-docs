KasPool

KasPool (bridge/kaspa/contracts/pool.ag) is a constant-product pool for KAS and kUSDT that runs as covenants on Kaspa L1. It is its own app, in its own covenant family, and it observes the kUSDT family. Its covenant ID is the deployment’s poolId; wallets pin it like kUSDT’s. Specification: SPEC §15.

Actor What it is
Pool A singleton. Its UTXO holds the KAS reserve, a fixed base deposit and the protocol’s accrued KAS. Its state also holds the circuit breaker and the pause
Position One per liquidity deposit: a number of shares and an owner key. Each carries 2 KAS

The pool’s kUSDT reserve is one KCC20 state in the kUSDT family, owned by the pool’s covenant ID (owner scheme 0x04), with borrowing disabled, holding Y + protocol_tokens.

Entries at a glance

Section titled “Entries at a glance”
Entry Does Checks (exact, with 128-bit products)
init First deposit The admin’s signature; X = Y = S = 0 before; S = kas_in; the Position gets kas_in − 1000 shares, and the 1000 unowned shares stay forever
buy KAS in, kUSDT out Not paused; fee 0.50 %, of which 0.05 % of the input goes to protocol_kas; (X + 0.995·in) · Y′ ≥ X · Y; the breaker’s clock age_daa
sell, sell_all kUSDT in, KAS out Not paused; the same, with the protocol cut in kUSDT; and the circuit breaker, with age_daa within the pool UTXO’s real age
add KAS and kUSDT in, a new Position Not paused; shares · X ≤ kas_in · S and shares · Y ≤ tokens_in · S; age_daa settles the breaker first, as a swap’s does; into an emptied pool (only the 1000 unowned shares left) the admin’s signature, and the reference starts again at the new reserve
remove Burns a Position (its owner signs) Open while paused; kas_out · S ≤ shares · X, tokens_out · S ≤ shares · Y; S stays ≥ 1000; age_daa settles the breaker first; while paused it claims 0 and settles nothing
tick Settles the breaker with the pool UTXO’s age, and nothing else Anyone; not paused; an opened pool (refused between the genesis and init); age_daa at least the minimum claim (50 DAA); no kUSDT input; the pool’s value unchanged
collect The admin takes protocol_kas and protocol_tokens Admin signature; X and Y unchanged
pause Stops swaps and new liquidity The guardian’s signature; not paused already; no kUSDT input in the transaction
unpause Resumes them The admin’s signature; no kUSDT input
set_keys Hands the admin key, the guardian key or both over Admin signature; no kUSDT input
Position.transfer Gives a Position to a new owner Owner signature; no kUSDT input in the transaction

The details of each follow.

State

Section titled “State”
Field Meaning
token_covid The kUSDT covenant ID
kas_reserve (X) KAS in the reserve, in sompi
token_reserve (Y) kUSDT in the reserve, in base units
total_shares (S) All liquidity shares, including the unowned minimum
protocol_kas, protocol_tokens The protocol’s accrued fees, kept apart from the reserves
base_value KAS the pool UTXO always keeps beyond the reserve (its storage deposit)
admin The key that collects protocol fees, resumes a paused pool and hands the keys over
max_out_bps, window_len The circuit breaker: the share of the KAS reserve that is the cap, and the DAA it takes one cap to refill. Fixed at genesis
window_credit How much of the room buys gave is still KAS those buys left in the reserve. A withdrawal moves it back onto window_out
Reserve deposit The reserve’s token output keeps at least 1 KAS, enforced by the pool: nothing else binds that KAS
window_ref, window_out The KAS reserve the cap is measured on (set by the first deposit, down at once with the reserve, up only with claimed time), and the allowance used so far in net KAS (sells out, less buys in, moving toward zero with claimed time; negative after net buying, at most one cap)
paused Whether the guardian paused swaps and new liquidity
guardian The key that may pause the pool, and nothing else: the bridge’s guardian

The circuit breaker

Section titled “The circuit breaker”

kUSDT minted with stolen bridge keys (3 of 5) is real kUSDT to the pool: the same token, with the same covenant ID. A forged mint and a sell into the pool can land in the same block, before any monitor sees them. So nothing off-chain can stop such a dump. The pool limits it on-chain instead.

Rule Value
Cap max_out_bps (1 000, 10 %) of window_ref, or of the reserve now if that is smaller. init sets window_ref; a sell or withdrawal lowers it at once. It rises only with claimed time: a swap moves it toward the reserve by age_daa ÷ window_len of the gap, so KAS that arrives at one moment (a buy, new liquidity) can’t lift it
Used window_out: the net KAS out so far, sells less buys. Only claimed time and real buys lower a positive count, and a withdrawal that shrinks the pool cuts it to two of the new caps, which gives no room. Negative after net buying, at most one cap
Reference window_ref rises only with claimed time, by at most its own size per window. Liquidity added to inflate the cap and withdrawn again earns nothing; a large genuine deposit lifts the cap by about doubling an hour
Refill One cap per window_len DAA (36 000, an hour at 10 BPS): window_out moves toward zero by elapsed × cap ÷ window_len, stopping at zero. Idle time never fills it past one cap
Buys Never capped. Each buy gives sells back the KAS it added to the reserve, at most one cap of credit. If that KAS is then withdrawn, the room goes with it
Clock Every public entry (buy, sell, add, remove, tick) names age_daa, the DAA since the pool UTXO it spends was accepted, at most two windows. Its pool input’s sequence must be at least that (OP_CHECKSEQUENCEVERIFY), and consensus holds a relative lock against the UTXO’s real age, so the clock can’t run ahead of real time. Every pool transaction makes a new UTXO of age zero, so claiming less only gives less room
Shortest claim While more than half of the cap is in use (window_out above half of the cap on the stored window_ref), every public entry claims at least window_len ÷ 720: 50 DAA, about 5 seconds. So nobody can spend the pool more often than that, every spend refills at least that much, and the count falls back to half a cap at the full rate. At or below half, sells have at least half a cap of room and a spend may claim nothing. A withdrawal while the pool is paused needs no minimum. A tick always claims at least 50 DAA. init, collect, pause, unpause and set_keys claim no age: the first comes before any window, the others take the admin’s or the guardian’s key
Bound At one moment: at most one cap of the reference net of buys, plus any credit, whatever sequence of buys, sells, adds and removes is used. Over any time t: about cap + credit + cap × t ÷ window_len, with cap on the largest reference in that time. Credit is at most one cap and comes only from real buys
Who can change it Nobody: both values are fixed at genesis

Then the monitor pauses the pool with everything else. A dump costs the liquidity providers at most about one cap (10 % of the KAS side) and then its refill rate, and they can still withdraw.

Time a spend does not claim is lost. Before 10 November 2026 any pool spend could claim none, so a bot sending a dust buy, or an add and a remove, every few blocks could keep a used-up allowance from refilling for its network fees alone. Now, while more than half of the cap is in use, every such spend claims at least 50 DAA, so spending the pool often refills it. The minimum stops at half of the cap so that dust can’t keep it in force: a first version applied it whenever any sell room was in use, and a bot could keep that true with a dust sell in every slot. Keeping the count above half takes selling about the whole refill, a cap an hour.

While the pool is paused the breaker stands still: no tick, and a withdrawal claims nothing and needs nothing, so providers can always leave at once. The admin’s unpause starts the clock again, so a long pause never hands sells a fresh cap.

The first deposit takes the admin’s signature: the pool’s opening, and the first deposit after every provider has left, which also starts the sell cap again at the new reserve. An opening by anyone with dust left a cap so small that sells stayed shut for about a day.

Slots

Section titled “Slots”

Above half of the cap, each pool UTXO can be spent once it is 50 DAA old: one slot per pool UTXO, which goes to whichever transaction the network takes first. Wallets and the web app are ready at that same DAA as anyone, so a bot has no head start. A bot that always gets there first can keep every other pool transaction out while the count is above half: with dust for at most as long as the count takes to fall to half (half an hour after a full cap was sold, an hour and a half if half the pool was also withdrawn), since its own spends refill at the full rate, and for longer only by also selling about a cap an hour, besides a fee per slot (720 an hour, about 42 KAS). In the tests a seller who wins one race in two loses about one race per sell and is served in full. Replacing others’ transactions with a higher fee spreads across the network, but a bot can always bid higher: it would only start a fee auction in each slot, so the clients don’t.

The keeper

Section titled “The keeper”

What is left is a bot that lets the pool UTXO age and then spends it claiming only those 50 DAA, so the count stays up. The keeper is a small service (kusdt keeper, with its own key, which pays only network fees). It follows the pool, and once it sees a spend that claimed less than its UTXO’s age by more than 5 seconds while more than half of the cap was in use, it sends a tick for the next hour 2.5 seconds after each new pool UTXO is 50 DAA old (so wallets, who send at that moment and take up to about 2.5 seconds to do it, come first), while more than half of the cap is in use, the pool is not paused and nobody else’s spend of that UTXO is on its way. A tick settles the breaker and changes nothing else, so the time it claims stays counted whatever the next spend claims; one that is refused or dropped is sent again. In the tests, a bot that spends each pool UTXO 95 seconds late keeps the refill at 5 % of the stated rate without a keeper and 84 % with one; a bot that stays just under the 5 seconds is never caught and keeps it at 50 % while nobody else spends (76 % with a seller about). The keeper’s 2.5 seconds are a trade-off: sooner, it took slots from slower wallets; later, a griefer landing just before it wastes more of each slot.

The keeper pays one coin per tick, free again as soon as that tick is gone, and splits a coin into four when it starts. A tick costs about 0.04 KAS at the network’s floor and 0.4 KAS at the keeper’s fee ceiling: at most 480 an hour (19 or 196 KAS), and only while more than half of the cap is in use. Without sells that lasts at most half an hour of claimed time after a full cap was sold, and an hour and a half if half the pool was also withdrawn (about 830 ticks). Anyone may run a keeper; the operators run one. It keeps its own coins and the pool’s age from the transactions the chain accepts, so coins anyone sends to its address or to the pool’s do not stop it (since round 13), and its health check fails, with the reason, when a tick it owes could not go out for a minute.

Wallets and the web app build a pool transaction only on a pool UTXO their node shows accepted and at least 50 DAA old while more than half of the cap is in use, and send it at that DAA. A node refuses a transaction whose relative lock is not met yet instead of keeping it, so they send again for a moment if their node is a little behind.

Why the clock is the UTXO’s age

Section titled “Why the clock is the UTXO’s age”

Until 8 November 2026 the breaker’s clock was now_daa, a claim bounded only from above by the transaction’s lock time. A seller could name any older time, so the clock could be set back and idle windows replayed one claim after the other: a day without sells gave 24 windows at once, and the first sell ever could count the chain’s whole history (92 % and 98.5 % of the reserve, in tests). The clock is now the pool UTXO’s own age, which consensus proves with a relative lock and which a new UTXO starts again at zero, so one moment can claim only the one age the current UTXO has.

Why the reference rises only with time

Section titled “Why the reference rises only with time”

The first refilling design reset the reference to the current reserve when a sell found no allowance in use. Then a buy of at least one cap reset it to a reserve that buy had just inflated, and a positive count was cut down to a cap that had shrunk, so buy two caps, sell all the room drained about 2 % of the reserve per cycle with no time passing (found in an independent review, 8 November 2026). Now only claimed time raises the reference, and a positive count falls only with claimed time or real buys. Time weights the reference by how long the reserve stood at that level.

Why it counts the net flow

Section titled “Why it counts the net flow”

Until 5 November 2026 the breaker counted every sell and ignored buys. An external review pointed out that anyone could then use up the hour’s sell room with round trips (buy kUSDT, sell it straight back) for their fees alone, and so hold up honest sellers. Counting the net flow ends that: a round trip brings in about as much KAS as it takes out, so it leaves the room where it was and only pays its fees, mostly to the liquidity providers.

It protects the pool just as well. On the constant-product curve the pool’s end state follows from its net KAS change alone. Whether a dumper sells at once, or buys first and then sells more, the pool ends at the same point, so the most it can lose at once is the same 10 % of its KAS. Tests in tests/pool.rs play both: five 900 KAS round trips leave an honest seller the whole room, and after a large buy the sells may take more than the cap gross but never more than one cap of credit past it.

The local end-to-end run (npm run local:e2e) shows it: three stolen signer keys mint 400 000 kUSDT and sell them into the pool at once. The breaker refuses the whole sell, and the most it lets out is under 10 % of the pool’s KAS. Within seconds the monitor pauses the pool.

Pause

Section titled “Pause”
Who Entry Effect
The guardian (the bridge’s, held by the monitor) pause Swaps (buy, sell, sell_all) and new liquidity (init, add) stop
The admin unpause They resume

remove stays open while paused, so liquidity providers can always withdraw, at once. The breaker stands still while paused (no tick; a withdrawal claims no time), and the unpause starts its clock again. The monitor pauses the pool on any alarm, with the vaults and the minter. A paused pool takes no second pause: the guardian’s key can’t keep moving a paused pool from under a withdrawal or under the admin’s unpause.

Before its first deposit the pool takes no tick either. Nothing is in it yet, and a tick would only move it to a new outpoint, away from where the launch check looks for it and from under the admin’s opening deposit, which may be signed offline. Until that deposit only the admin and the guardian can spend it.

Liquidity shares

Section titled “Liquidity shares”
Entry Rule
init (first deposit) Requires an empty pool. Sets the price as kas_in : tokens_in. total_shares = kas_in, of which the depositor’s Position gets kas_in − 1 000 and 1 000 belong to nobody. Requires kas_in > 1 000
add Adds kas_in and tokens_in for shares new shares, with shares × X ≤ kas_in × S and shares × Y ≤ tokens_in × S. The depositor gets a new Position
remove Burns a Position (its owner signs) for at most its share: kas_out × S ≤ shares × X and tokens_out × S ≤ shares × Y. At least 1 000 shares must remain, and both reserves stay above zero

init checks no key: whoever makes the first deposit into a new pool sets its starting price. Adding liquidity pays no fee. A Position can change hands with its owner’s signature (Position.transfer).

Why MIN_LIQUIDITY

Section titled “Why MIN_LIQUIDITY”

The first deposit mints 1 000 shares that nobody owns and nobody can withdraw, because remove always leaves at least 1 000 shares. In the contract’s words, they keep the share price from being manipulated while the pool is tiny. Separately, remove requires both reserves to stay above zero.

Exact 128-bit products

Section titled “Exact 128-bit products”

Kaspa script numbers are 64-bit, but reserve products are not: 1 billion KAS against 100 million kUSDT is 10¹⁷ × 10¹⁴ = 10³¹. KasPool therefore compares a × b ≥ c × d exactly, without overflow:

  1. Each operand must be between 0 and 2⁶¹ − 1.
  2. Each is split into 31-bit limbs: a = a1 × 2³¹ + a0.
  3. The partial products (a0 × b0, a1 × b0 + a0 × b1, a1 × b1) each fit in 64 bits.
  4. Carries are normalised so both sides have limbs below 2³¹, and the limbs are compared from the top.

A test runs a buy against 1 billion KAS and 100 million kUSDT and confirms that the quote passes and one unit more fails.

Why it cannot be sandwiched

Section titled “Why it cannot be sandwiched”

A swap transaction spends the exact pool UTXO it was built on, and it names the exact amount the user receives. A competing swap that lands first spends that UTXO, so the user’s transaction becomes invalid instead of executing at a worse price. Nobody can insert a trade before and after a signed swap.

The cost is contention: only one swap can spend a given pool state. When several users swap at once, all but one must re-quote and re-sign. Wallets build on the newest pool state, mempool included (the coordinator API serves it), and rebuild when it moves.

A front-runner can still make a wallet rebuild on a worse price. So wallets never take a worse price without asking: they go ahead on their own only if the new quote is at least the one you confirmed. A worse one needs your approval again. Your slippage minimum stays the hard floor.

Why every entry guards the reserve

Section titled “Why every entry guards the reserve”

The reserve is owned by the pool’s covenant ID. Under KCC-2’s covenant-id/v1 scheme, any input of the pool’s family in a transaction counts as the pool approving, and that includes a Position. So two rules close every path:

Entry Guard
Every Pool entry that moves kUSDT (init, buy, sell, sell_all, add, remove, collect) Observes the kUSDT family exhaustively: only the reserve and the one user state the entry’s shape allows may move, and the reserve’s successor is forced
Pool.pause, Pool.unpause, Pool.set_keys Require OpCovInputCount(token_covid) == 0: no kUSDT may move in the transaction
Position.transfer Requires OpCovInputCount(token_covid) == 0

Tests prove that a thief who owns a Position, or who holds the admin key, cannot move the reserve: the guard itself rejects the transaction.

Protocol fees and the admin

Section titled “Protocol fees and the admin”
Entry Who Effect
collect The admin key Takes all accrued protocol fees: the KAS leaves the pool UTXO to outputs the admin signs for, and the kUSDT goes to a fees state. Reserves are unchanged
unpause The admin key Resumes a pool the guardian paused
set_keys The admin key Hands the admin key, the guardian key or both to other keys (it replaced set_admin)

The admin can take only the accrued protocol fees, resume a paused pool and hand the keys over; it cannot touch X, Y, any position or the circuit breaker. Before mainnet, the operator holds the admin key offline (mainnet checklist, item 7).

Tests

Section titled “Tests”

Tests run the pool in Kaspa’s script engine (bridge/kaspa/tests/pool.rs). Each transaction spends the pool and its kUSDT reserve together, two covenant families, and runs input by input:

Area What is shown
First deposit Sets the price and locks the minimum liquidity; only once
Swaps Buys and sells pay at most the quote; one unit more is rejected; a round trip loses only the fees
Forced successors A pool claiming more tokens than the reserve keeps, a reserve keeping fewer than the pool records, or a pool forgetting the protocol’s cut is rejected
Fees Only the admin collects protocol fees
Reserve guards The reserve cannot ride along a Position transfer or an admin key change
Large numbers Exact 128-bit products with reserves whose product is far above 2⁶³
Liquidity Liquidity comes in proportion and leaves with its share; only the Position’s owner can burn it; Positions change hands with the owner’s signature
Circuit breaker The net outflow is capped at one cap per moment; round trips leave the room; a buy gives room back and a buy cannot forge the count; a full cap comes back after an idle window, half after half a window; the clock cannot run ahead of the pool UTXO’s age and claiming less only costs room; a burst at one moment can’t replay idle windows; the first sell ever has one cap whatever the chain’s age; withdrawals shrink the cap
The shortest claim and the tick While more than half of the cap is in use every public entry claiming under 50 DAA is refused, and at 50 accepted; at or below half (and for a withdrawal while paused) a claim of nothing is accepted; a tick settles only the breaker, is refused with any kUSDT in its transaction, with a changed pool value or under 50 DAA, and works while paused; every accepted public spend above half is a tick of at least 50 DAA followed by its own effect, and below half sells always had at least half a cap (a search over about 140 000 spends and 7 000 states); the review’s dust bot holds the slots only until half of the cap is back, through the real builders and the node’s rules; the slot race of every bot strategy, with and without a keeper
Pause and keys Only the guardian pauses and only the admin resumes; withdrawals stay open while paused; the reserve cannot ride along a pause or a key change; the admin rotates the guardian

tests/standard.rs builds the pool’s transactions with the real builders: a sell is about 108 000 transient mass, an add about 114 000. See Storage mass.

The full pool flow (genesis, first deposit, buy, sell, add, remove, collect) also ran on a kaspad v2.1.0 simnet.
