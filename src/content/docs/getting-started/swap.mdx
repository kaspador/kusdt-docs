KasPool is a KAS⇄kUSDT pool on Kaspa L1. It holds a reserve of KAS and a reserve of kUSDT and prices every swap
by the *constant-product* rule: after a swap, the product of the two reserves may not shrink. Each swap is a
single Kaspa transaction.

:::caution[Mainnet, launching today, not audited]
KasPool launches on Kaspa mainnet with kUSDT on 14 November 2026. Swap in the web app at
[trestly.io](https://trestly.io) once the launch is verified; until then it shows a "Launching today" page. The
pool's covenant ID is published on [Status](/overview/status/#addresses) at the same time: check it before you
swap. A new pool can be thin, so check the price impact in the quote before you sign. No
independent audit has been done ([Audit status](/security/audit-status/)).
:::

:::tip[In the web app]
The [web app](/getting-started/web-app/)'s Swap page quotes with the same core the covenant checks against, and
shows the 0.50 % fee, the price impact and your slippage setting. The wallet then builds and signs the swap.
:::

## How a swap works

1. **Get a quote.** Your app reads the pool's current state: the KAS reserve *X* and the kUSDT reserve *Y*.
2. **Build the transaction.** The app builds a transaction that spends the pool UTXO, the pool's kUSDT reserve
   and your own coins, and names the **exact** amount you receive.
3. **Sign and submit.** Your wallet signs your inputs. The pool covenant accepts the transaction only if the
   reserves after the swap satisfy the constant-product rule with the fee applied.

## Price and fee

Every swap pays **0.50 %** of its input: 0.45 % stays in the pool for liquidity providers, 0.05 % accrues to
the protocol. The best amount a swap can return is:

```text
effective = floor(input × 9 950 / 10 000)
output    = floor(reserve_out × effective / (reserve_in + effective))
```

For a buy, the input is KAS, `reserve_in` is *X* and `reserve_out` is *Y*. For a sell, the input is kUSDT,
`reserve_in` is *Y* and `reserve_out` is *X*. Asking for one base unit more than this quote is rejected.

| Pool | You send | You receive (quote) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 000 KAS and 1 000 kUSDT | 100 KAS | about 9.85 kUSDT |

A round trip (KAS to kUSDT and back) returns a little less than you started with, about the 1 % paid in
fees. You also pay the normal Kaspa network fee in KAS. The wallet shows about how much before you swap, at the
feerate its Kaspa node names, and sends nothing if the node names more than half as much again by the time the
swap is built. Nor does it send a swap that would leave the network change too small to keep (under 0.2 KAS, more
at a high feerate) beyond what was shown: it tries other coins, or asks you to merge your KAS or change the amount
a little. A buy of Max keeps 0.3 KAS for the fee, and the fee row then says "up to 0.3 KAS".

## No sandwiching, no slippage after signing

A swap transaction spends the exact pool UTXO it was built on and names the exact amount you receive. Three things follow:

- **Nobody can worsen your price after you sign.** If another swap lands first, it spends that pool UTXO.
  Your transaction then becomes invalid, rather than executing at a worse price.
- **You may need to retry.** In a busy moment your app may have to re-quote and re-sign against the new pool
  state.
- **The wallet asks before a worse price.** It goes ahead on its own only if the new quote is at least as good as
  the one you confirmed. Your slippage minimum stays the hard floor. kUSDT Wallet asks in its own window.

## Selling kUSDT

A sell is the mirror image. Your kUSDT state is an input, any remaining kUSDT returns to you as change, and the
KAS goes to an output you choose.

A very small sale can be refused. The network keeps change only from 0.2 KAS up, and the wallet's builders leave
less than that to the network fee. When you sell a part of a kUSDT state and hold no other KAS, what the sale
brings is that change: if less than 0.2 KAS of it is left after the network fee, all of it would go to the fee. The
wallet refuses such a sale before it signs anything and says why. Sell more, sell all of that kUSDT (the 1.5 KAS
under it then comes back with the proceeds), or hold some KAS first. It also refuses a sale whose network fee is
as large as what the sale brings.

### The sell limit

To bound a dump of forged kUSDT, the KAS sells take out of the pool, less what buys bring in, may be at most 10 %
of its KAS reserve per hour (the [circuit breaker](/contracts/kaspool/#the-circuit-breaker)). Buys are not limited,
and each buy gives sells back the KAS it brought. If your sell is over what is
left, the app tells you that the pool lets at most so much KAS out until about a given time. Sell less, or wait
for the next window.

## When the pool is paused

The bridge's guardian pauses the pool on any alarm, with the vaults and the minter. While it is paused:

| Stops | Keeps working |
|---|---|
| Buys, sells and adding liquidity | Removing liquidity: you can always withdraw your Position |

Only the pool admin resumes it.

## Providing liquidity

Anyone can add KAS and kUSDT in the current ratio and receive a **Position**: a covenant UTXO that records
liquidity shares and the key that owns them. Burning the Position returns at most its share of each reserve.
The owner can transfer a Position to another key. See [KasPool](/contracts/kaspool/) for the exact rules.
