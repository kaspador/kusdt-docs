Mainnet checklist

kUSDT does not go to mainnet until every item below is done. Mainnet deployments also need a funded deployer and the project owner’s explicit approval.

# Requirement Why Status
1 Five independent signer operators, each with their own nodes The federation is only as strong as the independence of its members Open
2 External audits of BridgeMint and KasPool (and the SilverScript they compile to) and of the vault No part is audited yet Open
3 The vault owner is an AccessManager whose admin is a Safe: 48-hour delays, about 6 for the guardian and unpause, and independent cancellers The owner is the one role not capped by the release allowance Open: the deployer builds it (mainnet:plan refuses anything else on mainnet); the Safe’s holders and the cancellers are still to be chosen
4 Legal review of holding users’ USDT The vaults custody user funds Open
5 Proof of reserves published live (INV-1) Anyone should be able to see that backing covers circulation Open
6 kUSDT’s genesis reviewed by kascov.io Wallets mark unreviewed tokens “unvalidated” Open
7 The pool admin key held offline by the operator It can take only the accrued protocol fees, unpause the pool and hand the keys over (KasPool) Open
8 A decision on redemptions to a blocked recipient Ethereum USDT can blacklist an address, and a payout there would be frozen Done (4 November 2026): a blocked recipient is never paid; the burn is held and paid if the address is unblocked. No re-target power exists. See Blocked recipients
9 Burn finality scaled to the payout A release cannot be undone Done (5 November 2026): 6 000 DAA above 10 000 kUSDT, 36 000 above 100 000; the preflight refuses shallower tiers

Operations

Section titled “Operations”

Not items of the specification’s checklist, but part of running the bridge safely:

  • the monitor running on its own nodes, with the guardian keys, and its GET /health wired to uptime alerts;
  • planned governance announced in the monitor’s expectedGovernance before the signers sign it, or the monitor pauses everything;
  • an operator ready to investigate and acknowledge alarms. Acknowledging never unpauses.

The runbook is on Operations.

From the attack review

Section titled “From the attack review”

The attack review adds these, in order:

  1. The pool’s circuit breaker. Done.
  2. No silent worse re-quotes in the wallet. Being built.
  3. At least 2 independent monitors, with external alerts on /health.
  4. Five independent signer operators (item 1), each with a pruned Kaspa node and independent EVM RPCs (see What a signer needs).
  5. External audits (item 2), and allowances sized to what can be lost. With 3 stolen keys, today’s limits risk about 500 000 USDT from the vaults, plus at most about 10 % of the pool’s KAS.

Rehearsal on mainnet forks

Section titled “Rehearsal on mainnet forks”

npm run fork:rehearsal (in bridge/devnet, passed on 3 November 2026) deploys the vault on local forks of Ethereum and BNB Chain against the real tokens: USDT (6 decimals, no ERC-20 return values) and BSC-USD (18 decimals). On each it runs a deposit, a 3-of-5 release, refusals of a replay and of misordered signatures, a refund of exactly the minted amount, a fee withdrawal bounded by the fees, a guardian pause and an owner unpause. It also checks the tokens’ blocklists: a release to an address Tether blacklists is paid and frozen there, a vault Tether blacklists pays nothing, and BSC-USD keeps no blacklist. Nothing touches a real chain.

Guard rails in the deploy script

Section titled “Guard rails in the deploy script”

The vault’s deploy script (bridge/evm/scripts/deploy.ts) enforces part of this on chain IDs 1 and 56:

  • it refuses the mock token;
  • it requires the USDT address and decimals from the specification;
  • it requires the owner to be a contract;
  • it asks for the confirmation KUSDT_CONFIRM_MAINNET=deploy-to-<chainId>;
  • it checks the hard bounds before sending anything.

The mainnet deployer (bridge/devnet/src/mainnet-deploy.ts, guide in deploy/mainnet-deployer) wraps it:

  • plan checks every input and only reads: the five signers’ keys (on the curve, in signer order), the guardian, the pool admin, the coordinator and the deployer, each key in one role only; the owners are contracts; the SPEC §8 limits, with every override named; the deployer can pay.
  • execute runs only the plan whose SHA-256 the reviewers confirmed, behind KUSDT_CONFIRM_MAINNET=deploy-kusdt-mainnet, with the plan’s deployer keys, and after the live checks pass again. It records each step and never deploys anything twice.
  • verify is for every operator: each vault’s token, signers in order, guardian, owner and limits, and the launch states against the scripts their UTXOs commit to.

npm run mainnet:rehearse (in bridge/devnet, with npm run local:stack up) runs the three commands against forks of Ethereum and BNB Chain that keep their chain IDs, so the vault deploy takes its mainnet path against the real USDT, and a simnet Kaspa node. It passed on 5 November 2026. It covered:

  • the refusals: a wrong plan hash, no confirmation, the wrong keys;
  • the deploy;
  • a second execute that sent nothing;
  • verify;
  • the five signers running, and each role’s preflight;
  • each vault’s AccessManager: the Safe’s direct calls revert, it pauses at once, its unpause waits the emergency delay, a canceller cancels a queued setSigners but not an admin call;
  • a live monitor with a canceller key: an announced call passes quietly, an unannounced setSigners is alarmed and cancelled, the Safe’s admin call is alarmed, the guardian pauses, and the integrators’ signal says stop.

The rehearsal for release candidate mainnet-v0.9.8-rc.7 passed all 48 checks.
