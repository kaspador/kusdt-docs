Coordinator API

Where it runs

The coordinator’s API process (kusdt api, bridge/services/src/api/) serves this API for the public testnet at https://testnet.trestly.io/api/v1, and in the local stack at http://127.0.0.1:9100/api/v1 (see Run the simnet flow). The mainnet base URL is to be published.

The API is public and read-mostly: JSON over HTTPS under /api/v1. Nothing in it moves funds. Every answer that matters can be checked against a node: a token state against its UTXO, a pool state against its script.

Amounts are JSON integers, and some exceed 2⁵³, so clients must parse them exactly (not as JavaScript numbers). A value of 16 or more digits can also arrive as a decimal string, because the coordinator reads its saved state that way: the minter’s remaining is one. genesisRemaining, every breaker and exact value, and each tier’s above are always strings. Accept both forms. In a request body, the coordinator reads an integer exactly in any JSON notation: 12345678901234567, 12345678901234567.0 and 1.2345678901234567e16 are the same integer. The public testnet’s services may lag this page (see Status).

Where the answers come from

Section titled “Where the answers come from”

Since the work after round 12 the API is answered by a process of its own beside the coordinator, never by the coordinator itself: a flood of requests can at worst slow or stop these answers, and costs the process that mints and pays nothing. That process reads what the coordinator writes: its state and token index, its archive of closed records, and a public copy written once a second (the pool and the minter as followed now, how far each chain was read, the coordinator’s last health check). It asks the node and the EVM RPCs over connections of its own. The paths, the fields and the status codes are the ones they always were. How old an answer can be:

  • GET /health is the coordinator’s last health check, with its own at. It answers 503 when the public copy is more than 15 seconds old: the coordinator is stopped, restarting or cannot write, and every answer here is that old.
  • GET /pool answers 503 with retryAt once the copy is more than 15 seconds old, rather than hand a wallet a pool state the coordinator stopped updating. Within that bound it is the coordinator’s newest state, as before.
  • The records (deposits, redemptions, positions, the reserves’ figures, the minter in /status) are as of the coordinator’s last save, a few seconds behind what it knows. Every output /tokens and /positions list is still looked up on the node when it is answered.
  • Before the coordinator has written anything, the routes that need its files answer 503 with retryAt.

The coordinator learns the minter, the pool and the positions the way the monitor does: from accepted transactions, checking every successor against the outputs (see Following covenant states). It takes supply from the chain:

Calls

Section titled “Calls”
Call Answer
GET /status {network, kaspa: {covenantId, poolId, virtualDaa, minter: {utxo, state}, genesisRemaining, tokenValue, burnValue, finalityDaa, finalityTiers}, chains: [{chain, name, chainId, vault, token, decimals, scannedBlock, depositNonce, backing, unreservedBacking, reservedUnits, releaseAvailable, paused, fees: {feeBps, minDepositFee, minReleaseFee, minDeposit, maxDeposit, releaseCapacity, releaseRefillPerSecond}, blocklist}]}. Each vault’s parameters as it reports them. blocklist is the token view the bridge honours (isBlackListed), or null for a token that keeps none (BSC-USD). scannedBlock is the last final block the coordinator read (finalizedBlock is its old name)
GET /tokens?owner=<kaspa address or x-only hex> {states: [{utxo, state}], unread?}: the owner’s unspent kUSDT states. unread (since rc.19) lists apart the coordinator’s records of the owner’s states at an address that holds too many coins for the node’s answer to be read at all, with the KAS each coin carried as recorded: the node said neither that they are there nor that they are gone, so they are never in states (see Known limits). Answers 400 for an owner that is neither.
GET /pool {pool, reserve, pending: [txid], confirmed: {pool, reserve}, prices: {kasInKusdt, kusdtInKas}, exact: {unitsPerKas, sompiPerKusdt}, breaker, paused, tvlKas}. pool and reserve (each {utxo, state}) are the newest, mempool included, carried through the pool moves in pending (only moves a block can take: a pool transaction that no block will include is never listed, see Known limits); confirmed is the newest accepted. exact: {unitsPerKas, sompiPerKusdt} gives the spot prices as integer strings, rounded down (kUSDT units per KAS, sompi per kUSDT); prices are floats for display. breaker: {maxOutBps, windowLen, maxKasOutNow, windowEndsDaa, virtualDaa, poolDaa, minAgeDaa, readyAtDaa} is what sells may still take out of the KAS reserve now (see the circuit breaker): poolDaa is the DAA score the pool UTXO was accepted at (null while it is only in the mempool, and for a moment while the coordinator does not know it yet, see Request limits), minAgeDaa the least age a pool transaction must claim now, and readyAtDaa the DAA score before which none can be sent (null while the newest pool UTXO is only in the mempool, or while its age is not known and a claim is needed). paused says whether the guardian paused the pool. priceKas remains as an alias of prices.kasInKusdt. Answers 404 until the pool exists.
GET /positions?owner=<kaspa address or x-only hex> {positions: [{utxo, state: {tokenCovid, shares, owner}}], unread?}: the owner’s live liquidity positions, each found in the node’s UTXO set; unread as for /tokens. Answers 400 for an owner that is neither.
GET /deposits/:chain/:nonce {chain, nonce, txHash, blockNumber, logIndex, sender, ownerScheme, owner, units, fee, minted, status, kaspaTxid?, refund?}. A nonce the coordinator has not read yet answers only {chain, nonce, status: "pending"}. refund appears once Kaspa skipped the nonce: {chain, nonce, status, skipTxid, since, evmTxHash?, attempts?, nextTry?, lastError?}, with status skipped → refunded, or blocked while the token blocks the depositor (see Refunds). :chain is the code (1, 2), ethereum, bsc or bnb, or the deployment’s chain name; an unknown chain answers 404, and a nonce that is not a decimal integer 400
GET /recipients/:chain/:address {chain, address, blocklist, blocked, payable, reason}: whether the vault would pay address. payable is false for the zero address, the vault’s own address, and an address the token blocks; reason says which, or is null. Answers 503 when the token can’t be read, and 400 for an address that is not 0x and 40 hex digits. Wallets refuse a burn unless payable is true
GET /deposits?sender=0x… or ?owner=… {deposits: [{chain, ...}], total, limit, offset}: the matching deposits, newest first, at most 1 000 per answer. limit and offset page them; with no filter an answer holds 100 unless limit says more. sender is an EVM address (0x and 40 hex digits, in any case): anything else matches no deposit and gets an empty page
POST /redeems Body {transaction, transactionId} (a submitted burn in node JSON, with the ID the node answered) or {txid, outputIndex, chain, address, state} (the preimage of a burn without payload). state is {amount, owner, ownerScheme, borrowScheme, borrowGuard, extensionCommitment}. Answers {redeems: [{redeemId, status}]}. A malformed body answers 400, and one above 512 kB answers 413 (see Request limits). See Reporting a burn for what a report can and cannot do
GET /redeems/:txid/:outputIndex {redeemId, txid, outputIndex, chain, address, units, state, status, firstSeen, fee?, payout?, evmTxHash?, readyAt?, queuedTx?, attempts?, nextTry?, lastError?, note?, unverified?, checkedAt?, candidates?}, or 404 if no such redemption was seen. unverified marks a claim only a client has made so far, and candidates holds other claims waiting for the same outpoint. status queued means the payout is waiting in the vault: readyAt says until when (unix seconds) and queuedTx is the transaction that queued it. fee and payout come from the vault’s Released event; attempts, nextTry and lastError show the relayer’s retries, or why a payout is held
GET /reserves {supply: {minted, released, pendingBurns, circulating}, backing: {<chain name>: units, total}, at: {<chain name>: block}, healthy}, where healthy means total ≥ minted − released (INV-1). Each vault’s backing is read at the block its releases were counted to (at), so a release that just landed never looks like missing backing. circulating is minted − released − pendingBurns. pendingBurns counts burns not yet released, leaving out invalid records and claims neither the chain nor the node has shown, so a client’s word cannot move it. at is null for a vault whose releases have not been counted yet.

Status values

Section titled “Status values”
Object Statuses, in order
Deposit pending → final → minting → minted, or refunded
Refund (refund.status) skipped → refunded, or blocked (held while the token blocks the depositor; back to skipped once it doesn’t)
Redemption seen → final → signing → released, or invalid (not in the UTXO set), or unpayable (at or below the release fee, back to final if the fee falls below it; or a zero or vault recipient, for good), or blocked (held while the token blocks the recipient; back to final once it doesn’t)

A blocked record is checked again every hour (blockedRecheckMs). It never holds up other records. See Blocked recipients.

A payout the vault’s owner cancelled keeps its status, and its lastError says that it waits until the owner reinstates it. The coordinator asks no signer for it and sends nothing. It looks again every ten minutes and goes on once the owner has reinstated it. It holds up no other record.

Which burns are recorded. Only a plain burn is recorded as a redemption: a state with no borrowing and no extension, and an amount above zero. That is the signers’ own rule for a burn they would pay. A plain burn at or below the release fee is recorded and marked unpayable; the coordinator marks all of those at once, so they never take a real burn’s turn.

Request limits

Section titled “Request limits”

A request body is at most 512 kB (413 above it), and must arrive whole within 10 seconds or its connection is cut. The server reads at most 64 bodies at a time, and 4 from one client: more answers 429. A request’s headers must arrive within 10 seconds and the whole request within 30. These limits are separate from the limit of requests per minute per client, which an operator turns on in the API process’s config (apiClientIp). A proxy in front can add its own: the mainnet package’s Caddy allows bodies up to 1 MB and limits how long a request may take to arrive.

The read routes have limits of their own (they kept the API process’s own answers going before the split, and do so still):

  • /tokens and /positions read the owner’s addresses on the node. One read per owner at a time, shared by everyone who asks; the answer is kept for a second, or for twenty times the time its read took, up to a minute. All these reads together have a budget of time. Past it an owner asked about lately gets the last answer with stale: true, and anyone else 503 or 429 with retryAt.
  • /pool reads no address. The pool output’s age (poolDaa) is the DAA score at which the coordinator’s own follower saw that output accepted, and it keeps it in its state (and its public copy). For an output it did not see accepted (the first start after an upgrade from before rc.18, a pool that has not moved since the launch, a reorg that undid a pool move) the pool’s address is read once, in the background, by the API process: until that read is back, /pool answers poolDaa: null, which claims no age, and it never waits for it. What it learns stays in the API process: the coordinator takes no word from the process that faces the public.
  • Everything the API asks the Kaspa node goes over connections of its own, in a process of its own. Its reads of addresses wait in a line of their own, at most eight of them (503 with retryAt past that). The loops’ reads never wait behind them.
  • The archive (closed deposits and redemptions, on disk) is read within a budget of time; 503 or 429 with retryAt past it. A list read from it is kept for 30 seconds at the most.
  • What is kept in memory is bounded by the records it holds and by its age: the owners’ answers (100,000 outputs in all), the lists read from the archive (20,000 records, an empty list counting as one), the pages of /deposits (20,000 deposits).

Verifying answers

Section titled “Verifying answers”
  • A token state from /tokens names its UTXO. Recompute its locking script with kusdt-tx script and compare it with the UTXO on your own node. The wallet does exactly this before it shows or spends a state.
  • A pool state from /pool, and a position from /positions, can be checked the same way.
  • /reserves can be checked against the vaults’ backing(), read at the blocks in at, and the minter’s remaining. healthy compares the backing with everything minted and not yet released, so a burn still waiting for its release still counts as owed.

The monitor computes the same supply figures from its own nodes and does not use this API.

Reporting a burn

Section titled “Reporting a burn”

POST /redeems tells the coordinator about a burn it has not seen on its own, with its payload’s chain and address. The API process checks the body’s shape and holds the claim until the coordinator fetches it, which it does itself, at most five times a second and eight claims at a time, within the budget of time a claim always had. At most 32 claims (4 MB) wait at once, each for 15 seconds: past any of these, and while the coordinator is not asking (it is stopped or restarting), the answer is 503 with retryAt, and nothing is kept. A wallet asks again; a burn nobody reported is still found on the chain. A report is a claim, not a fact:

  • A transaction a client posts can only add redemption claims. Nothing proves it is the transaction with that id, so it never changes the coordinator’s token index.
  • Nothing is read from the node on the request. A claim for an output the coordinator’s own follower has read from an accepted transaction (the exact state, with kUSDT’s covenant ID) is a burn at once and replaces whatever came first. Any other claim waits, and a loop of its own tries every waiting claim against the follower’s records and then against the node’s UTXO set; the one found wins. Before round 11 the claimed state’s address was listed on the request itself: an address of the caller’s choosing, holding any number of coins.
  • A burn the coordinator reads from the chain itself always replaces a different claim.
  • Unproved claims are capped. The coordinator keeps at most 256 redemptions on a client’s word alone; past that, the oldest goes. A claim already found does not count, and an honest burn loses nothing, since the chain shows it anyway. Each redemption keeps at most 16 other claims, and each pass looks up at most about 128 claims on the node, after everything the chain has shown. A claim that came to nothing stays visible for an hour.
  • Unproved claims are not supply. They are left out of pendingBurns in GET /reserves until the chain or the node shows them.

So reporting a wrong state first cannot strand someone else’s redemption (fixed 5 November 2026). Signers re-check every burn on their own nodes before they sign, whatever the coordinator says.
