Limits

kUSDT limits what can move on both sides of the bridge. The limits cap the damage a bad signer quorum could do, and they are enforced by the contracts themselves.

Defaults

Section titled “Defaults”
Limit Default (mainnet) Enforced by
Kaspa mint allowance 500 000 USDT ceiling, refills fully in 24 h BridgeMint
Kaspa hard cap 1 000 000 000 USDT; it can only fall BridgeMint
Vault release allowance 250 000 USDT per vault, refills fully in 24 h KusdtVault
Deposit size 10 to 100 000 USDT KusdtVault
KasPool sells 10 % of the KAS reserve at once, refilling at that much per hour (net of buys) KasPool

The vaults’ maxDeposit must stay at or below the Kaspa allowance ceiling, so that every deposit can be minted. The vault cannot see the Kaspa ceiling, so this is checked by whoever changes either value.

At the mainnet launch

Section titled “At the mainnet launch”

Mainnet starts lower than the defaults, to match what each signer agreed to sign in a day:

Limit At the launch
Each signer’s daily limit (its fuse) 25 000 kUSDT per 24 hours, for mints and for releases each
Deposit size 10 to 8 000 USDT
Vault release allowance A ceiling of 25 000 USDT per vault, which refills fully in 24 h
Deposit meter, for the rising deposit fee 12 500 USDT per vault, half the signers’ limit; 2.50 % at a full meter
Kaspa mint allowance and hard cap As the defaults

The vault’s allowance is not a cap on a day. It is a bucket that holds at most 25 000 USDT and refills at 25 000 a day. After a quiet time the bucket is full: 25 000 can be queued at once, and it keeps refilling, so about twice 25 000 can be queued in the 24 hours that follow. Counting payouts already waiting in the queue from before, just under three times 25 000 can leave a vault in 24 hours (see Most that can move in a window). That is all the vault itself enforces, and it is the bound that counts if three signer keys are stolen.

The signers’ own limit does hold per 24 hours. Each signer counts what it signed in the last 24 hours and signs nothing that would take it past 25 000 kUSDT of releases and refunds, both chains together (and the same for mints). A payout needs 3 signers, so with honest signers no more than 25 000 USDT of payouts is signed in any 24 hours, whatever the vaults’ buckets hold.

Why they go together. A signer signs nothing that would take it past its daily limit, so:

  • a mint carries up to 3 deposits, and must fit: the largest deposit is at most a third of the signers’ limit. A deposit no signer would mint would also hold up every later deposit on its chain;
  • the web app and the wallets refuse a burn above the vault’s release allowance, so the allowance is at most the signers’ limit: a larger one would let someone burn kUSDT that no signer would pay out;
  • the two vaults’ deposit meters together are at most the signers’ limit, or the limit would be used up before the deposit fee reached its top rate.

The deployer refuses a plan that breaks any of these rules. Deposits and burns over a day’s limit are not lost: they wait until the signers’ 24 hours allow them, and a batch of deposits too large for the limit is split. Each signer’s limit is one budget for both chains, so the coordinator keeps what frees for the oldest deposit or payout that only the limit is holding up: the chain whose next deposit has waited longest goes first, and younger deposits and payouts wait. That wait ends once the oldest one has gone through, and after 25 hours at most.

Raising them later. The signers raise their dailyLimit in their own config (a payout needs 3 signers with room, so the bridge’s limit is the third highest). The vaults’ limits change through the vault owner, with the usual 48-hour delay. Raise the signers’ limit first, then the vaults’.

Kaspa’s DAA score rises about 10 times per second, so 864 000 DAA scores is about 24 hours.

How an allowance works

Section titled “How an allowance works”

Both allowances are buckets. A bucket holds up to its capacity. Each mint or payout takes from it, and it refills at a fixed rate:

Kaspa mint allowance Vault release allowance
Clock DAA score, proved by the transaction’s lock time block.timestamp
What draws from it The total of a mint A release’s whole units (the fee too: it leaves the backing as well) and a refund’s units
When empty The mint is refused until it refills The call reverts until it refills
Also The queue of payouts waiting holds at most one allowance: a payout that would take it past the ceiling reverts until what is queued is paid or cancelled
New deployment Starts as set at genesis Starts full

Most that can move in a window

Section titled “Most that can move in a window”

An allowance holds at most its ceiling and refills at ceiling ÷ 24 h. A full bucket can be spent at once, and it keeps refilling. So over any window of length t, at most capacity + refill_rate × t can be drawn from it. On Kaspa a draw is a mint, so the most that can be minted in any 24-hour window is twice the ceiling: a full bucket, then a day of refill.

A vault’s allowance is drawn when a payout is queued, and the payout leaves after its wait. Payouts queued before a window can be paid inside it, so a queue adds to the bound: at most capacity + refill_rate × t + one queue can leave a vault in a window of length t. The queue is capped at one ceiling, so that is just under three times the ceiling in any 24 hours: the queue held from before, a full bucket, and a day of refill less the last wait.

Window Kaspa mint allowance Each vault’s release allowance
At once 500 000 USDT 250 000 USDT
Any 24 hours at most 1 000 000 USDT (2 × the ceiling) under 750 000 USDT (just under 3 × the ceiling)
Sustained, per further 24 hours 500 000 USDT (the refill) 250 000 USDT (the refill)

The hard cap

Section titled “The hard cap”

remaining starts at 10¹⁵ units (1 billion USDT) and goes down with every mint. No entry raises it, including governance. It also keeps every Kaspa sum far below the 64-bit integer limit.

The pool’s circuit breaker

Section titled “The pool’s circuit breaker”

The KAS side of KasPool has its own limit. At most 10 % of its KAS reserve (the cap, max_out_bps) may leave through sells at once, net of buys, and that allowance refills at one cap per window_len DAA (36 000, an hour at 10 BPS). Idle time fills it to one cap and no further. At one moment, whatever mix of buys, sells and liquidity moves is used, one cap (plus any credit from earlier buys whose KAS is still in the pool) is the most that can leave net; over any time t about cap + credit + cap × t ÷ window_len. Its clock is the pool UTXO’s own age, which consensus proves with a relative lock, so it can’t run ahead of real time or replay idle hours, and the reserve the cap is measured on rises only with that claimed time.
